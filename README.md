**A FOUNDATIONAL PAPER · v3.0** 

# **Legibility Bias** 

How Formal Institutions Reallocate Risk, Credential Value, Labor, Moral Liability, and Economic Surplus — and How the Population is Conditioned to be Sorted 

A multi-site analysis of administrative burden, credentialism, and institutional betrayal across the university, the corporation, the journal, and the school. 

Synthesis of witness, documentary record, and theoretical framework 

## **Abstract** 

Formal institutions allocate benefits and burdens through standardized documents: medical notes, credentials, references, abstracts, placement statistics, performance records, aid formulas. This paper argues that when the availability of such documentation diverges from the underlying condition it is meant to describe, institutions systematically allocate by the document rather than by the condition. I call this legibility bias, and I specify the three conditions under which it is predicted to occur: a heterogeneous population, standardized documentary verification, and high stakes. I define the institution's aperture as the intersection of what its instruments can detect, what its procedures can process, and what its principals can audit, and I argue that the gap between the institution's purpose and its aperture is where reallocation occurs. 

The mechanism is structural rather than intentional: accountability demands require demonstrable evidence, standardized documentation is the cheapest demonstrable evidence, and the result is a systematic privilege of the documentable over the non-documentable that operates even when the institution's purpose is to serve the underlying condition. The mechanism is reinforced by an interest structure: those who control the interpretive categories also control the aperture, and widening it redistributes interpretive authority. 

I develop a five-channel typology — risk, credential value, labor, moral liability, and economic surplus — across four sites: the university, the corporation, the journal, and the school. The first three sites are visible within a single institutional frame; the last two channels become visible only when the university and the corporation are analyzed as one connected arrangement hinged on the credential, and the fourth site (mass schooling) is added as the developmental precondition that conditions the population to be sortable by the other three. I also recover the original seventeen-angle diagnostic taxonomy as a complementary lens to the five channels, and I show that the theory applies to itself: the "no loss" claim that gender quota research reports is itself an instance of legibility bias operating inside the research, with the formal labor market as its aperture. 

At the university site I document five verified cases in which psychological crisis was routed into disciplinary or exclusionary channels while physical injury was routed into accommodation, add a Hong Kong case in which the pattern was judicially ratified, and add a Louisiana case in which the gap between the official record and the underlying work appears as two grade books. At the corporate site I document the market in manufactured references and its spillover cost, the prevalence of ghost postings, H-1B extraction under fissured employment, the for-profit college feedback loop, and the documented economics of administrative absorption and debt transfer. At the publishing 

site I show that the abstract functions as an interpretation filter, that post-hoc review is temporally homologous to the fit-to-sit doctrine, and that publication converts a probability statement into a settled fact. At the school site I show that mass schooling conditions the population through learned helplessness, the extinguishing of the daemon (in Plato's sense of innate creative capacity), and a biological mismatch in the timing of credential sorting relative to capacity development. That site is presented as the developmental precondition for the other three. 

I engage counter-cases — a Finnish teacher quota that improved allocation, a university policy revision compelled by civil-rights enforcement, and four counter-configurations where the theory predicts no bias — and I respond to eight objections, including one concerning the paper's own use of reported cases and one concerning whether the paper is merely cynical. The central claim is that the sites share a common failure mode, not a common cause: an aperture calibrated to documentary evidence rather than to the underlying condition. The formal arrangement simultaneously expels real value and retains fake value, and its measurement framework is too narrow to detect either movement. The affirmative pole is that the aperture is a design choice, and design choices can be changed; the alternatives exist and function across four registers (policy, institutional design, research, pedagogy). The task is not to abandon legibility but to widen the aperture. 

##### **SECTION** 

### **§1 — Introduction** 

In 2004, a nineteen-year-old honors student at Hunter College of the City University of New York was hospitalized after a suicide attempt. When she returned to campus, she found the locks on her dormitory room changed. She was permitted to collect her belongings only under the supervision of a security guard, and she was then evicted. The college's stated reason was that her attempt posed a liability risk and violated a housing policy mandating automatic eviction for self-harm. Her attorney, David Goldfarb, stated the asymmetry plainly: had she been hospitalized for mononucleosis or pneumonia, he was confident she would have been welcomed back to her dorm. The college settled for $65,000 and abandoned the policy only after litigation. 

The case is not isolated. A straight-A sophomore at George Washington University who checked himself into hospital for depression after a close friend's suicide was barred from his dormitory within hours, charged with endangering behavior while still hospitalized, and threatened with arrest for trespassing if he returned to campus. A Yale freshman who sought medical attention after parasuicidal behavior was forced to withdraw; according to her published account, the evaluating psychiatrist told her, we just can't have you here. Between fall 2012 and spring 2017, a Department of Justice investigation found, Brown University denied readmission to dozens of undergraduates who had taken medical leave for mental-health reasons even when their treatment providers confirmed they were ready to resume study; the university paid $684,000 and revised its policies under ADA Title III enforcement. Quinnipiac University imposed mandatory medical leave on a student diagnosed with depression without considering reasonable accommodations, and settled for $17,000 in emotional distress damages plus $15,126.42 in tuition reimbursement. 

The puzzle is not that institutions sometimes decide harshly. It is that the harshness is systematically distributed by the form of the evidence rather than by the severity of the condition. A student with a fractured tibia — discrete, medically documentable, cognitively sparing — can present a note and receive reconstitution of tuition and a clean academic slate. A student living under coercive control, narcissistic abuse, or intimate harassment — conditions that consume cognitive capacity far more thoroughly than a healing bone — is typically unable to produce the documentation the institution requires, at the moment it requires it, in the format it recognizes. The institution does not see the second student's condition. It sees the absence of a note. 

This paper argues that the asymmetry is structural. It is produced by a mechanism I call legibility bias: the systematic tendency of formal 

institutions to allocate benefits and burdens according to the availability of standardized, verifiable documentation rather than according to the underlying condition, when the two diverge. The mechanism is not malicious. It is a rational response to audit pressure — the demand, from legislatures, boards, shareholders, funders, and accreditation bodies, that institutional decisions be demonstrable. Demonstrable evidence is cheapest in standardized documentary form. Institutions therefore privilege what can be documented over what cannot, even when their purpose is to serve the underlying condition. 

Two further cases, both reported rather than independently verified, extend the argument into territory the verified record cannot reach on its own, and they are introduced here because they are the paper's best answers to its most likely objections. The first forecloses the bad-apples reading. At the Chinese University of Hong Kong, a student with depression and generalized anxiety disorder was terminated after failing to meet the university's Maximum Study Period; she had submitted psychiatric reports and requested an extension. The court ruled against her, finding the Maximum Study Period amply justified and requiring statistical evidence of disproportionate impact on disabled students. This case is different in kind from the five above. It is not a settlement and not a finding of wrongdoing: the institution was vindicated. That is precisely what makes it important. It shows that legibility bias need not be a failure to follow the rules — the rules themselves can be built around the aperture, and the aperture can be lawful. The second makes the aperture visible as an object. In Louisiana, a National Board Certified Teacher nominated for teacher of the year was ordered by her principal to assign Ds to failing students. When she objected at a teachers' meeting, the principal told her to shut up and not open her mouth again. She kept a private grade book recording the grades she believed were accurate, while the administration altered the official record. She developed cardiac problems from the stress and filed suit. Two grade books, one accurate and private, one official and altered: this is the aperture not as metaphor but as filing cabinet. 

These two cases, and the reading they license, are returned to in §4.3 and §4.6. The contribution is sixfold. First, a concept. Legibility bias is defined precisely and bounded by three conditions — heterogeneous population, standardized documentary verification, high stakes — so that it can be falsified. Second, a mechanism. The six-step process in §3.5 connects the concept to the audit-society literature (Power 1997) and the administrative-burden literature (Herd and Moynihan 2018), and adds an interest structure the audit account alone does not supply: those who control the interpretive categories have an interest in the aperture remaining narrow, because widening it redistributes interpretive authority. Third, a typology of five channels. Risk, credential value, labor, moral liability, and economic surplus. The first three are visible within a single 

site. The last two are visible only when the university and the corporation are analyzed as one arrangement hinged on the credential, and their inclusion changes the paper's central formulation: the arrangement simultaneously expels real value (honest talent into unmeasured informal work) and retains fake value (manufactured credentials, ghost demand, inflated placement statistics), and its measurement framework is too narrow to register either movement. 

Fourth, a multi-site analysis across four sites. University extenuatingcircumstances governance, corporate credential screening, scholarly publishing, and mass schooling. The test is deliberately asymmetric. The university, corporate, and school sites are supported by documentary records and carry the evidentiary weight. Publishing is supported by a thinner corpus and is presented as illustrative; §6.6 states this limit, and the prediction table in §3.7 separates theoretical prediction from evidential confidence. The school site is added as the developmental precondition for the other three: it is where the population is conditioned to be sortable by the apertures that the university, the corporation, and the journal will later operate. Fifth, the recovery of the original seventeen-angle diagnostic taxonomy as a complementary lens to the five channels. The seventeen angles preserve the diagnostic richness of the source critique that the fivechannel abstraction compresses; they are presented as a reference table for researchers encountering the failure mode in domains this paper does not analyze in depth. Sixth, a meta-application: the theory applies to itself, and the no-loss claim that gender-quota research reports is itself an instance of legibility bias operating inside the research, with the formal labor market as its aperture. 

Three scope bounds must be stated at the outset. First, legibility bias is not universal. It is predicted when all three conditions are jointly present and predicted absent when any is missing; configurations without documentary verification — small-seminar grading, craft apprenticeship — are the theory's comparison cases. Second, the sites share a failure mode, not a common cause in the strong sense; §8.5 maintains the distinction. Third, the alternatives discussed in §11 are design principles, not a tested program, and the raw-findings protocol appears only in Appendix E, marked untested. The roadmap follows the paper's structure. §2 reviews the literatures and states the gap. §3 specifies the mechanism. §4 documents the university site. §5 documents the corporate site. §6 treats publishing illustratively. §7 documents the school site as developmental precondition. §8 compares the sites, specifies the phase cycle, and restates the commonfailure-mode claim, and adds the meta-application of the theory to itself. §9 engages the counter-cases. §10 responds to eight objections. §11 states implications. 

##### **SECTION** 

### **§2 — Theoretical Framework** 

The framework draws on ten literatures and adds four supplementary sources required by the school site. Scott's Seeing Like a State supplies the foundational concept: modern states pursue legibility — the rendering of populations and territories into standardized, simplified formats observable from the center. Legibility is not a neutral record of preexisting reality; it implies a remaking of that reality, because the state's categories replace the local, situated knowledge they purport to describe. Scott is necessary but not sufficient. He is concerned with the state and with projects of taxation, conscription, and control. The extension required here is to formal institutions generally — universities, employers, publishers, schools — which face the same imperative without being state actors. What the extension adds is a distributional question Scott does not pose: when an institution's aperture narrows to what is documentable, who becomes visible and who disappears? Scott documents the state's blindness to local knowledge; this paper documents the institution's blindness to illegible need. 

Herd and Moynihan supply administrative burden as the learning, compliance, and psychological costs individuals experience in interacting with the state. Their account is demand-side: burdens fall on citizens. The question here is supply-side: what determines which burdens an institution is willing to lift? The answer is documentary legibility. A burden is lifted when the claimant can produce standardized evidence at the moment the institution demands it. The psychological costs Herd and Moynihan describe are precisely what the notification window and fit-to-sit doctrines impose, but they do not theorize the evidentiary filter that determines who can clear it. Power's audit-society account supplies the causal engine: accountability demands produce documentation demands; documentation demands produce an aperture; the aperture determines allocation. What Power does not supply is the multi-site comparative application, nor the interest account of why the aperture resists widening. 

Collins supplies the bridge between the university and corporate sites. Credential inflation creates demand for signals separable from competence; once credentials become threshold commodities rather than reliable signals, the rational actor invests in signal manufacture rather than skill acquisition. The reference market described in §5.2 is not a deviation from Collins's credential society but its logical extension. What Collins did not anticipate, writing in 1979, is the industrial scale of contemporary manufacture: commercial reference farms, ghost postings, and summary fiction as routine professional practice. Smith and Freyd supply institutional betrayal as harm perpetrated by an institution upon individuals dependent on it, including failure to prevent or respond supportively to wrongdoings. The later courage literature adds the crucial qualification: courage 

attenuates the trauma symptoms associated with betrayal, but only when institutions act with transparency and accountability. Institutional courage is not compassion and not apology. It is the construction of an audit trail that can register what the old one could not. This is why Brown's postsettlement policy revision counts and a public statement would not, and it is the basis for the design principle in §11.1. 

Lipsky explains how legibility bias is operationalized. The notification window is not self-enforcing; it is enforced or waived by an advisor whose discretion is constrained by audit pressure. Waiving without documentation creates an audit trail the advisor must justify. Legibility bias is therefore a property of the interaction between rules and discretionary enforcement under audit conditions, which is why the paper's unit of analysis is the aperture rather than the policy text. Weil supplies the structural condition for the corporate reference market. The shell company, the parental business, and the contractor intermediary are fissure entities: they occupy the space between formal employment and the informal economy, oversight is thin, and the capacity to issue credentials is intact. The fissure is the institutional habitat in which credential manufacture becomes rational, and Weil explains why the lead firm has no incentive to close it — the fissure is where labor cost is displaced and brand value preserved. Gray and Suri supply ghost work as the honest informal economy: the self-taught developer, the freelance translator, and the platform worker are engaged and productive but illegible to credentialist institutions. Their invisibility is structural: the platform's legibility depends on the worker's illegibility. 

Shay and Litz et al. supply moral injury as the cost-side concept for the neutralization phase. The educator asked to inflate grades, stage photographs, and ignore learning deficits — and isolated when they refuse — is not burned out. They are experiencing the distinctive harm that follows from being forced to transgress values under institutional pressure. The concept locates the pathology in the institution's demands, not the individual's resilience. Practitioner-facing analyses have warned explicitly against labelling teachers' appropriate distress as a mental-health issue rather than a workplace betrayal, on the ground that doing so fragilizes the workforce and lets the system off the hook. The warning matters here for two reasons. It shows that the mechanism is recognized by the people inside it, which blunts the objection that the paper is reading pathology into ordinary workplace unhappiness. And it supplies the correct framing: the ethical educator's distress is a response to betrayal, not a deficit of coping, and treating it as the latter is itself an instance of the reallocation the paper describes. The socialization literature adds a complementary mechanism: the transmission of interpretive frames to new practitioners. A newcomer is taught the field's dominant frameworks before encountering primary evidence; the frameworks organize what counts as a finding; and the newcomer's work is evaluated against the frameworks rather than against 

the data. This is a legibility effect operating inside the profession itself: what the field can recognize is what the field's categories can accommodate, and the categories arrive before the evidence does. 

The disability-accommodation literature supplies the comparative model that makes legibility bias visible as a choice rather than a necessity. Disability-services research documents that documentation requirements effectively authorize accommodations for students who lack traditional medical proof, particularly students with cognitive disabilities, and that institutions may need to accept documentation from sources outside of the traditionally sanctioned ones. The OECD's work on recognition of prior learning (RPL) supplies the labor-market analogue: a formal mechanism for validating skills acquired through non-formal and informal learning. RPL exists because credentialist screening cannot see demonstrated competence, and it functions as an aperture-widening device in exactly the sense this paper specifies. This literature establishes that alternative verification is administratively feasible. The aperture can be widened. The existence of functioning alternatives is the empirical basis for the claim that legibility bias is contingent, and for the counter-cases in §9. 

Four supplementary sources are required by the school site (§7) and by the engagement-performance alternative (§11.4). The shadow education literature (Bray; Asian Development Bank) documents that private supplementary tutoring maintains and exacerbates educational inequality because prosperous families purchase greater quantities and better qualities of tutoring; in India, 25.9 percent of school dropouts in Delhi slums cited inability to afford private tutoring as the reason for withdrawal, and parents and students believed that attending school without tutoring yielded no positive result. The finding establishes that a remediation market grows around the gap between what the institution certifies and what the student can do, and that the market's revenue depends on the gap persisting. Marcel Mauss's The Gift supplies the framework for the kinship reference market in §5.2: total prestation describes exchange in which goods, services, and recognition circulate together and create binding obligation. The kinship reference market is a total prestation — the reference is given, the subservience is returned, and the debt is never discharged because discharging it would end the relationship on which future references depend. This is why the transactions leave no paper trail: they are not cash transactions but social-debt transactions, and social debt is precisely what an audit cannot see. 

Seligman's learned helplessness literature supplies the developmental frame for the student population the school produces. The classical experiments describe how organisms that learn their actions cannot affect outcomes cease to act, even when escape becomes available. The translation to the classroom is direct: a student whose curiosity is treated 

as disruption, whose compliance is rewarded over creativity, and whose agency is managed rather than cultivated learns that agency is not available inside the institution. The student internalizes this as the structure of adult behavior and carries it into the career, where he then manages other people's agency the same way. Learned helplessness is not a side effect of mass education. It is the explicit product. The school is a training ground for the credentialed incompetent who will later fill the empty-room strategy, the fake-reference cartel, the LinkedIn potlatch, and the abstractworshipping journal. Finally, Plato's daemon, genie, and divine spark supply the interpretive frame for what the school extinguishes. The daemon is the unique inner spark of creative insight that cannot be taught but must emerge from engagement. The genie is the capacity to create something new. The divine spark is the innate wisdom and pattern recognition, the ability to see connections others miss. When the paper says that schools extinguish these qualities through confinement rather than cultivation, it is describing a system that treats creativity as dangerous or disruptive rather than as essential for solving real problems. The school does not merely fail to cultivate the daemon. It actively trains it out, through years of compliance reinforcement, through the punishment of deviation, through the reward of performance over engagement. 

Taken together, the literatures establish four things. First, legibility is a general institutional imperative, not a state-specific one, driven by accountability demands that produce audit rituals. Second, legibility has costs, distributed through learning, compliance, and psychological burdens, enforced through frontline discretion, and experienced as betrayal and moral injury when institutions respond to need with suspicion; the courage literature adds that what repairs betrayal is not expression but changed recording. Third, credentialism is the specific form legibility takes in the transition from education to employment, operating through fissured organizational forms that allow signal manufacture at scale while rendering honest informal labor invisible. Fourth, alternatives exist: the aperture is a policy choice, and shadow education, learned helplessness, Mauss's total prestation, and Plato's daemon supply the additional frames required to extend the analysis to the developmental precondition the other three sites require. What the literatures do not provide is a unified account across sites. No framework specifies the common mechanism by which the same legibility imperative produces homologous reallocations of risk, credential value, labor, moral liability, and economic surplus across university governance, corporate hiring, scholarly publishing, and mass schooling. That is the gap this paper fills. 

**SECTION** 

### **§3 — The Mechanism** 

Legibility bias is the systematic tendency of formal institutions to allocate benefits and burdens according to the availability of standardized, verifiable documentation rather than according to the underlying condition, when the two diverge. Three components must be kept distinct. It is a tendency, not a rule: the institution does not always prefer the documentable, but does so with sufficient regularity to be predictable. It concerns allocation of both benefits and burdens: legibility bias is not only about who receives help; it is equally about who is made to carry institutional risk. It is conditional: where document and condition coincide, the bias does not operate. The definition is deliberately neutral about motive. Whether any administrator intends the pattern is an empirical question, not a definitional one. The claim developed in §3.5 is that the pattern is largely structural and that it is reinforced by an interest structure that is not structural in the same sense but is equally real. 

One further point follows from the definition and deserves emphasis, because it is the claim the Hong Kong case makes vivid. Legibility bias is not defined by illegality. An institution can operate a narrow aperture while fully complying with its own rules, and a court can find those rules justified. The bias is a property of the allocation, not of the institution's compliance record. This is why the theory cannot be tested by counting settlements. Three conditions must be jointly present. First, heterogeneous population: the institution serves populations whose needs are not uniform and cannot be captured by a single evidentiary format. A university serves students whose incapacities range from a fractured tibia to coercive control. A corporation recruits candidates whose competence ranges from pedigree to self-taught demonstration. A journal serves readers whose interest ranges from a single finding to a theoretical reframing. A school serves children whose capacities range from creative engagement to compliant performance. Second, standardized verification: the institution allocates on the basis of documentary evidence — medical notes, credentials, references, abstracts, placement statistics, performance records, aid formulas. Standardization makes verification administratively feasible; it also creates the aperture. The document becomes the operative fact. Third, high stakes: the allocation materially affects life outcomes — tuition, continuation, employment, publication, housing, immigration status, developmental trajectory. High stakes convert a bureaucratic inconvenience into a reallocation. 

The five-channel typology was derived initially from the sites, then expanded when the corporate and university sites were analyzed as one connected arrangement. The first three channels — risk, credential value, moral liability — are visible within a single site. The last two — labor and economic surplus — become visible only when the university and the 

corporation are analyzed as one arrangement whose hinge is the credential. Risk reallocation: when an institution lifts the burden of failure only for those who can document incapacity, it transfers residual risk of failure onto those who cannot. The student with a fracture receives restitution; the student in coercive control receives the penalty. The institution has not eliminated risk; it has sorted it. Credential-value reallocation: when allocation runs through credential rather than demonstrated competence, a market for credential manufacture emerges. The self-taught developer is filtered out; the manufactured-reference candidate is filtered in. The institution believes it is measuring competence; it is measuring the legibility of competence-claims. 

Moral-liability reallocation: when an institution responds to internal harm by isolating or pathologizing the reporter, responsibility moves from perpetrator to victim. The educator who refuses to inflate grades is reassigned to a role with no duties; the group member who refuses to participate in a plagiarism trap is framed as a co-conspirator. Labor reallocation: this channel is visible only across sites. The credential's function as threshold creates two opposite movements of labor. Honest movement: skilled actors who cannot clear the credential screen exit into the gig economy, open-source networks, and self-taught markets — engaged, productive, and invisible to the measurement framework. Corrupt movement: unskilled actors who cannot demonstrate competence instead manufacture the document and remain inside the formal system, formally accounted and substantively unproductive. The ILO's estimate that 58 percent of global workers are informally employed, with men at 60 percent and women at 55 percent, describes the honest side. The reference market describes the corrupt side. The institution's aperture cannot see either, because it reads the document and not the labor. 

Three features of the labor channel deserve separate statement. Unpaid and reproductive labor: caregiving, household maintenance, and community coordination are productive and are not counted by market measures of engagement. An actor who withdraws from formal employment to perform them is registered as disengaged, and the work they perform is registered not at all. This is the purest case of the labor channel: not exit into informal paid work, but work that never enters the measurement frame in any form. Non-documentary assets: networks, sponsorship, reputation, and the goodwill of colleagues are productive assets that no credential certifies and no form records. Where an aperture allocates by document, these assets are invisible both in their presence and in their loss. Non-random segmentation: the two movements of labor are not randomly distributed. Which actors exit and which manufacture depends on resources: capital to buy a manufactured reference, social capital to obtain one through reciprocity, or the absence of both. The aperture therefore does not merely sort the documented from the undocumented; it sorts along lines that are already 

structured by prior advantage. The Greater Male Variability Hypothesis adds a statistical mechanism to the labor channel that the original aperture framework did not specify. If the distribution of talent is more variable for men than for women — more at the extremes, both high and low — then average effects (no overall loss) can mask a significant loss of extreme talent at the top end. A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis in the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology examined whether the GMVH in creativity can adequately explain the gender gap in creative achievement. The implication for the labor channel is precise: the aperture does not merely conceal composition; it conceals the tail. The most skilled exiters are also the least visible, because their exits are read as outliers rather than as the population the theory predicts. The aggregate measure reports no loss because the aggregate is dominated by the center; the tail, which is where the loss actually is, disappears into the unmeasured informal economy without trace. 

Economic-surplus reallocation is the fifth channel and is also visible only across sites. When the credential functions as a threshold, the institution that issues it can charge for issuance independently of the value delivered. The mechanisms are documented in §5.6: administrative absorption of tuition and aid, and the transfer of risk to individual borrowers. The consequence is that the arrangement extracts surplus from students and transfers it to institutional administration, while the credential's signal value is diluted by manufacture. The two-direction formulation is the central claim of the typology. The formal arrangement does not merely fail to measure value. It expels real value — honest talent into unmeasured informal work — and retains fake value — manufactured credentials, ghost demand, inflated statistics — and it cannot detect either movement because both are invisible to an aperture calibrated to documents. 

The display signature is a behavioral pattern the theory predicts wherever allocation is by legible credit rather than substantive contribution: effort migrates from the task to the display of the task. A coerced research participant who completes a survey as quickly and loweffort as possible while attempting to appear diligent is spending energy on the display rather than the participation. The same signature appears in the student who submits degraded work under trauma and thereby certifies fitness to be assessed, in the employee who posts a sophisticated role description while performing none of the role's functions, and in the group that invests in the appearance of shared authorship rather than in the work. The signature is not cynicism. It is the rational response of an actor who has correctly understood which artifact the institution is reading. The aperture is the institution's capacity for registration — what it can actually see, process, and defend. The aperture is not the institution's purpose. The stated purpose of extenuating-circumstances policy is that grades reflect competence rather than misfortune. The aperture is narrower: what the 

institution can register. The gap between purpose and aperture is where reallocation occurs. The method is to read the aperture off the administrative artifacts — forms, deadlines, documentation categories, formulas — rather than off the stated purpose. The aperture is also the reason the paper's central image is a record rather than a decision. A decision is an event; a record is an object. Two grade books in Louisiana, an altered placement statistic in a for-profit college, a submitted assignment that waives a claim: in each case the reallocation is carried by an artifact that can be filed. The theory is about what gets filed. 

The causal mechanism operates in six steps. Step 1: accountability demand. Institutions face demands from principals — legislatures, boards, shareholders, funders, accreditation bodies. The university must demonstrate that extenuating-circumstances decisions are consistent; the corporation that hiring is merit-based; the journal that publication is rigorous; the school that students are progressing. Step 2: demonstrableevidence requirement. A principal cannot audit an administrator's private judgment; it can audit a file. The demand for accountability is a demand for documentation. Step 3: standardized documentation is the cheapest demonstrable evidence. A doctor's note is standardized; a narrative of coercive control is not. A degree is standardized; a repository of demonstrated work is not. An abstract is standardized; the dataset is not. A grade is standardized; a description of what the student actually understands is not. The institution does not choose standardization because it is epistemically superior; it chooses it because it is administratively efficient under audit conditions. 

Step 4: systematic privilege of the documentable. The consequence is that the institution privileges what can be documented even when its purpose is to serve the underlying condition. The privilege is operational, not psychological. Step 5: systematic reallocation. Benefits flow to the documentable; burdens fall on the non-documentable. The reallocation is the aggregate output of thousands of discretionary decisions, each locally rational, none requiring malice. Step 6: interest reinforcement. The audit account explains why the aperture is narrow; it does not fully explain why it stays narrow when the cost of narrowness is visible. A sixth step is required, and it is an interest account rather than a structural one. Those who control the interpretive categories have an interest in the aperture remaining narrow, because widening it redistributes interpretive authority. Senior researchers control which frameworks count as legitimate and therefore which findings are legible; their authority is bound to the frameworks. Administrators control which documents count as valid; their discretion is bound to the documents. Widening the aperture is not merely an administrative change; it is a redistribution of authority, and it is resisted for that reason. This step explains what Step 5 cannot: why reform, where it occurs, tends to arrive from outside the institution — from 

litigation, from enforcement, from a new principal imposing a new demand — rather than from internal review. The interest reinforcement also operates through prior population conditioning, not only through current authority. A population trained for years to accept credential as proxy for competence, compliance as proxy for engagement, and performance as proxy for understanding has internalized the aperture as natural. The interest in maintaining the narrow aperture is then distributed across the population itself, not concentrated in any single authority. This is the developmental layer of Step 6, and it is the reason the school site (§7) is not a fourth instance of the failure mode but the precondition that produces the population the other three sites sort. 

The dynamic consequence: an actor who is honest today and is filtered out by the aperture faces a choice between exit into unmeasured work and manufacture of the document. Exit is available and is what most actors choose — which is why the honest informal economy is large. But exit removes the honest actor from the pool the aperture can see, and manufacture inserts the dishonest actor into it. Over iterations, the visible pool becomes more manufactured and less honest, not because actors are corrupting but because the aperture has been selecting. This is the reason the cycle does not self-correct: the aperture's own operation degrades the signal it reads, and the degradation is invisible to the aperture because the aperture measures the signal's presence rather than its provenance. 

The theory is falsifiable. Six criteria would refute it. First, equal treatment of illegible and legible conditions in a measured context: if psychological trauma received treatment equivalent to physical injury in a university with the same audit architecture, the prediction fails for that site. Second, inverse correlation between legibility and benefit: if more legible conditions were less likely to receive accommodation, the central prediction is reversed. Third, no improvement under reduced legibility: if an institution that adopted narrative or interview-based verification showed no change in allocation, the aperture would not be doing the causal work attributed to it. Fourth, no cross-site homology: if the four sites showed no structural similarity once the three conditions were held constant, the commonfailure-mode claim fails. Fifth, no interest effect: if widening were adopted internally at the same rate as externally, Step 6 would be doing no work and the structural account alone would suffice. Sixth, no lawful instances: if every documented instance of the pattern turned out to involve an institution violating its own rules, the claim that legibility bias is a property of apertures rather than of non-compliance would fail. Criterion 6 is the one the reported Hong Kong case addresses. If the pattern were always a matter of institutions breaking rules, it would be a compliance problem, and the remedy would be enforcement. If the pattern can be lawful — if a court can uphold a maximum study period against a student with psychiatric 

reports — then the remedy must be a change in what the rules require an institution to register. 

The measurement corollary is the paper's answer to the no-loss defense that institutions routinely offer. Aggregate outcome measures conceal compositional reallocation. An institution can report that average outcomes are unchanged — the same proportion of students graduate, the same proportion of claims are approved, the same proportion of candidates are hired — while the composition of who receives the benefit has shifted entirely toward the documentable. Averages are insensitive to composition; composition is what the theory predicts. The Greater Male Variability Hypothesis sharpens the corollary: averages are also insensitive to distributional extremes. The tail, where the most acute loss is concentrated, is averaged into the center, and the aggregate reports no loss because the loss is in the part of the distribution the aggregate does not represent. The aperture also mistimes measurement. The school issues the credential — the high-school diploma, the standardized test score, the grade — at a developmental moment (around age eighteen) when the capacity the credential claims to measure is not yet mature. Prefrontal development continues into the mid-twenties and, on some accounts, into the midthirties. The credential is therefore issued before the capacity it claims to measure has developed, and the aperture's timing claim becomes a form of legibility bias in itself: the credential sorts at the moment the population is most developmentally vulnerable to being sorted wrong. This is an aperture-timing claim, not a neuroscience claim; the institutional point is about the timing of sorting relative to the development of capacity, not about the neural mechanism of capacity itself. Any test of the theory must therefore measure who clears the aperture, not merely how many. The two grade books make this concrete. The official record is the aggregate: it reports grades, and it reports them at a rate. The private book is the composition: it records who earned what, and it records that the official rate is not a description of the class. An institution that reports only aggregate rates has not tested the theory, because the theory predicts precisely that aggregate rates will be the last thing to move — and, where records can be altered, the thing that moves last is the record itself. 

The prediction table separates theoretical prediction from evidential confidence. At the university extenuating-circumstances site, all three conditions are present, the theoretical prediction is strong, and the evidential confidence is strong on five verified cases, three verified policy samples, and DOJ settlements, with two reported cases extending the analytical reach. At the corporate credential-screening site, all three conditions are present, the theoretical prediction is strong, and the evidential confidence is strong on ghost-job surveys, the H-1B False Claims Act matter, for-profit records, and reference-market reporting. At the journal peer-review site, all three conditions are present, the theoretical 

prediction is strong, but the evidential confidence is moderate-to-weak, supported by architectural observation and limited sourcing; the site is illustrative rather than demonstrative. At the mass-schooling site, all three conditions are present (heterogeneous population, standardized verification via performance records and grades, high stakes including life trajectory), the theoretical prediction is strong, and the evidential confidence is moderate, supported by the shadow education literature, the learned helplessness tradition, and the biological-mismatch timing argument; prevalence data on the outcomes of mass schooling are not claimed. The counter-configurations — small-seminar grading, craft apprenticeship, lowstakes administrative forms, homogeneous compliance checks — are the theory's falsification conditions in comparative form. If small-seminar grading and craft apprenticeship showed the same allocation pattern as the high-stakes documentary sites, the mechanism would not be doing the explanatory work. 

The Seventeen Angles of Epistemological Incompleteness, recovered from the source critique as a diagnostic taxonomy, complement the five channels. The channels describe what moves; the angles describe where to look for the movement. They are presented as a reference table for researchers encountering the failure mode in domains this paper does not analyze in depth. The streetlight effect (Angle 1) maps to the aperture construct itself: researchers look for what is missing only where it is easiest to measure. The credentialist bias (Angle 2) maps to the corporate channel: self-taught talent is invisible to credentialist screening. Alternative credentialing (Angle 3) maps to the labor channel: GitHub portfolios and open-source contributions are productive but unmeasured. Informal leadership (Angle 4) maps to the moral-liability channel: community organizers are invisible. Narrow productivity (Angle 5) maps to the measurement corollary: wages as flawed proxy. Greater male variability (Angle 6) supplies the statistical mechanism for the tail-concealment claim. The paradox of quotas (Angle 7) is the source critique's specific target: quotas can create paradoxical effects not captured by engagement metrics. Self-stigmatization (Angle 8) describes how the apertured population internalizes the aperture's verdict. The quota ghost (Angle 9) describes how the threat of an aperture change alters behavior before any change occurs. Spillover effects (Angle 10) describe how the aperture degrades signals for the entire category, not only for those filtered. Network displacement (Angle 11) describes how the apertured lose access to networks controlled by the unapertured. Efficiency loss (Angle 12) describes how effort declines under apertured conditions. Backlash (Angle 13) maps to the neutralization phase: hostility toward those who expose the aperture's operation. The meritocracy assumption (Angle 14) describes how the aperture's defenders assume the pre-aperture measure was accurate. Unpaid labor (Angle 15) maps to the labor channel: caregiving invisible. Self-selection (Angle 16) 

describes how the apertured and the unapertured select different strategies. Future uncertainty (Angle 17) describes how engagement is dynamic: an honest actor today may become a manufacturer tomorrow. The seventeen angles do not falsify the five channels; they diagnose the same failure mode from different observation points. A researcher studying, for example, the spillover effect of fake references on honest candidates (Angle 10) is studying the credential-value channel; the angle specifies where to look, the channel specifies what moves. 

The meta-application: the theory applies to itself. The original source critique was specifically a critique of gender quota research's no-loss claim — the assertion that quotas do not reduce male engagement or productivity. The claim is not, strictly speaking, empirically false. It is epistemologically incomplete: a conclusion that is true only within the narrow measurement boundaries that researchers have chosen. The quota researchers' aperture was the formal labor market — formal employment, formal wages, formal tournament performance. They reported no loss because they measured no loss. The ILO's finding that 58 percent of global workers are informally employed, with men at 60 percent, describes the population their aperture could not register. The honest informal economy — the gig workers, the open-source developers, the self-taught programmers, the community organizers — is invisible to the formal-employment aperture. The no-loss claim is legibility bias operating inside the research itself. This is not a complaint about quota research. It is the theory's strongest demonstration of its own reach. The same aperture logic that produces the asymmetry at the university and the corporation and the journal and the school produces the no-loss claim inside the research that studies the asymmetry. And the theory applies to this paper as well. The paper has its own aperture: the verified / reported / witnessed evidentiary registers. The paper's own aggregate — eleven cases, seventeen angles, five channels, four sites — conceals its own composition: which cases are verified, which angles are diagnostic, which channels are site-derived. The paper's response to this self-application is not to deny it but to make the registers visible (§11.6). The wager is that making one's own aperture visible is the precondition for changing it — and that this is the discipline the paper demands of institutions and therefore must demand of itself.

##### **SECTION** 

### **§4 — Site 1: The University** 

The university combines all three conditions in sharp form. The population is heterogeneous; verification is documentary; the stakes are high. The stakes are severe in a specific way: the sanction is not a grade adjustment but a status change — eviction, suspension, forced withdrawal, denial of readmission. The extenuating-circumstances apparatus is the mechanism by which such status changes are supposed to be prevented. The architecture within which this occurs must be stated. Tuition is structured not as a fee-for-service with guaranteed delivery but as a personal sacrifice underwritten by the student, who pays in advance for a 

program whose completion depends on thresholds whose conditions the student does not control. Failure carries exclusion from continuation and, in many programs, a multi-year ban before re-enrollment in the same program is permitted. For a young student the ban is not a scheduling inconvenience; it is a rupture in the career timeline and a devaluation of prior investment. 

The allocation question is therefore sharp: on what basis does the institution lift the burden of failure? The stated answer is that grades and costs should not be distorted by unforeseen circumstances. The operative answer, the paper argues, is documentary. A physical injury has a date of onset, a treating professional, a standardized document, and a recovery timeline. A note converts it into an administrative fact the institution can process, file, and audit. On that basis a student can obtain reconstitution of a year's tuition and relief from academic consequences. Two features matter. First, the note is cheap to produce and verify, which is why it functions as the paradigm of admissible evidence. Second, the injury is frequently cognitively sparing. A student with a healing fracture may be unable to walk easily while retaining full capacity to draft an appeal, correspond with an advisor, and meet a filing deadline. The condition that qualifies the student for relief leaves intact the capacity required to claim it. Physical legibility and preserved advocacy capacity coincide. 

Psychological crisis is documented across five verified cases. At Hunter College in 2004, the honors student was hospitalized after a suicide attempt and found her locks changed on release; the rationale was that the attempt posed a liability risk and violated a policy mandating automatic eviction for self-harm. The case settled for $65,000; the policy was abandoned only after litigation. Goldfarb's counterfactual is the cleanest available statement of the differential: the same apparatus that accommodates a physical hospitalization treats a psychiatric one as a conduct problem. At George Washington University, Jordan Nott, a straight-A sophomore, checked himself into hospital for depression after a close friend's suicide. Within hours he received a letter barring him from his dormitory; the following day, while still hospitalized, he was charged with endangering behavior under the student code of conduct. He was suspended, evicted, and threatened with arrest for trespassing if he returned. He withdrew and completed his degree elsewhere; the Bazelon Center filed suit and the case settled confidentially. The sequencing is analytically decisive: the conduct charge was issued while the student was an inpatient. The institution did not wait for recovery and then assess; it converted the act of seeking treatment into the violation. 

At Yale in 2013, a freshman sought medical attention after parasuicidal behavior and spent a week in hospital. She was forced to withdraw. According to her account, the evaluating psychiatrist said: the truth is we 

don't necessarily think you'll be safer at home, but we just can't have you here. The quotation concedes the institution's own uncertainty about the welfare rationale while stating the operative reason: the institution's inability to accommodate her presence. The aperture did not fail to detect the condition; it detected it and declined to process it. At Brown, between 2012 and 2017, the DOJ investigation found that the university denied readmission to dozens of undergraduates who had taken medical leave for mental-health reasons, even when their treatment providers confirmed they were ready to resume studies, and required students to remain on leave for at least two semesters regardless of medical recommendations. Brown paid $684,000 and revised its policies under ADA Title III. Brown is the decisive case because it isolates the mechanism from the question of whether the students were well. The treatment providers — the professionals whose documentation the physical-injury regime treats as authoritative — confirmed readiness, and the institution denied readmission anyway. The relevant evidence was available and was not the operative fact. 

At Quinnipiac, the DOJ settled allegations that the university imposed mandatory medical leave on a student diagnosed with depression without considering reasonable accommodations, requiring $17,000 in emotional distress damages plus $15,126.42 in tuition reimbursement. The violation was not the leave itself but the failure to ask whether the standard process could be adapted. The ADA framework requires that question; the administrative process did not ask it. The gap between legal requirement and administrative process is the aperture. The lawful aperture: the Chinese University of Hong Kong case. A student with depression and generalized anxiety disorder was terminated after failing to meet the university's Maximum Study Period. She had submitted psychiatric reports and requested an extension. The court ruled against her, finding the Maximum Study Period amply justified and requiring statistical evidence of disproportionate impact on disabled students. This case is different in kind from the five above, and its difference is the reason for including it. The institution was not found to have done anything wrong. It applied its rule; the rule was challenged; the rule was upheld. If the argument of this paper required institutions to be breaking their own rules, the Hong Kong case would be a counter-example. It is not, because the argument does not require that. The case shows that an aperture can be built into the rules themselves and can survive judicial review. The student's psychiatric reports were, on the court's reasoning, not the right kind of evidence: what was required was statistical evidence of disproportionate impact. The aperture was thus not a failure of the institution to look; it was a specification of what looking would consist of. 

Two implications follow. First, the bad-apples reading of the pattern is foreclosed. The five US cases can be read as institutions behaving badly; the Hong Kong case cannot, and it exhibits the same allocation. Second, the 

remedy cannot be compliance. An institution that follows a narrow rule perfectly is not less biased than one that follows it imperfectly. This is what criterion 6 of the falsifiability section tests. Two grade books: the Goudreau case. In Louisiana, a National Board Certified Teacher nominated for teacher of the year was ordered by her principal to assign Ds to failing students. When she objected at a teachers' meeting, the principal told her to shut up and not open her mouth again. She kept a private grade book recording the grades she believed were accurate while the administration altered the official record. She developed cardiac problems from the stress and filed suit. The image is the paper's most literal. The aperture is usually described as a filter, which is a metaphor. Here it is an object: a book, kept in a drawer, that contains the composition, alongside an official record that contains the aggregate. The two are not in disagreement about a number. They are in disagreement about what the class is. And the institution's obligation runs to the second book, because the second book is the one that can be filed. The case also belongs with the for-profit record in §5.6, and the parallel should be stated plainly: Goudreau's two books are the micro-form of the institutional falsification schemes. When an administrator at a forprofit college deleted a graduate's low earnings and wrote a new record from an average salary website, the structure was the same. The difference is scale, not kind. 

The second mechanism is temporal. Policies require notification at or near onset and invalidate late claims. At Goldsmiths, University of London, applications must be submitted no later than 7 working days after a deadline or examination, and late applications will be rejected, unless the student can demonstrate they were prevented from disclosing the circumstances at the time. At the University of Galway, ongoing circumstances must be notified normally no later than 7 days before the submission date of the first scheduled assessment; after that, only professional evidence of inability to notify earlier is accepted. At the University of Portsmouth, the fit-to-sit doctrine forecloses the claim entirely once work is submitted on time. The trap is structural, and the conditions that make early notification hardest are precisely those the policy is meant to accommodate. Consider what the rule requires of a student under coercive control. It requires that the student recognize the condition as a condition — possess the frame and the distance to name what is happening. It requires that they disclose to an institutional officer, which requires a safety judgment a person under coercive control is frequently unable to make. It requires that they do so within days of onset, when the characteristic temporal structure of trauma is delayed recognition. And it requires that the disclosure be articulate — that it take a form the advisor can record and the dean can audit. 

The Galway clause is the most revealing: late notification is permitted only where the student can supply professional evidence that they could 

not, or were not in a position to, have notified the College office earlier. The exception requires the student to have been in contact with a professional at the moment of incapacity — but impaired capacity to seek professional contact is the defining feature of the condition. The exception is written for the cases the rule already anticipates; the cases it cannot anticipate are the ones it most needs to reach. The window has a second effect that is not visible in the outcome data because it operates before any claim is filed. A student who knows the rule — who has read the policy, or has heard from a peer that late claims fail — may decline to seek accommodation at all. The rule does not need to be invoked to shape behavior; its existence is sufficient. This is a general property of documentary regimes: anticipation of the requirement shapes conduct before the requirement is applied, and the effect is systematically invisible because the actors it suppresses never appear in the record. It is the reason outcome studies understate the aperture's reach: they measure the claims that were made, not the claims that were abandoned. 

The fit-to-sit doctrine at Portsmouth converts a behavior into a declaration. By submitting, the student has made a statement about capacity; the statement is not evaluated but inferred from conduct. The burden of proof is allocated at precisely the wrong moment. A student capable of submitting is deemed capable of having been assessed — even where submission was accomplished at the cost of the capacity the assessment was meant to measure. And a student not capable of submitting within the window has already failed the notification requirement. The doctrine makes the appearance of coping the operative fact. A student who, under coercion or trauma, submits degraded work has certified that no accommodation is needed. The aperture registers the submission and nothing else. The student is penalized not for failing but for partially succeeding, because the partial success is legible and the cost is not. This is the display signature in its most consequential form: the energy spent producing the submission is energy not spent recovering, and the submission itself is read as evidence that no recovery was needed. This is where several channels intersect most visibly. Risk is reallocated: the student carries the academic consequence of degraded performance. Credential value is reallocated: the work is treated as a valid signal when it is not. Moral liability is reallocated: inability to perform is converted into an implicit representation of capacity, absolving the institution of any obligation to inquire. Economic surplus is reallocated: the tuition is retained while the service the tuition purchased — assessment of real competence — is not delivered. 

The paper's witness material (Appendix C) describes an educationalservices employer that refused a proposal to map students' actual learning deficits and isolated the educator who made it. The witness is not offered as prevalence evidence. What is offered as evidence is the structural reason 

the proposal was refused, and that reason is documented in the shadoweducation literature. Private supplementary tutoring maintains and exacerbates educational inequality because prosperous families purchase greater quantities and better qualities of tutoring. In India, 25.9 percent of school dropouts in Delhi slums cited inability to afford private tutoring as the reason for withdrawal; parents and students believed that attending school without tutoring yielded no positive result. The finding is not that tutoring is harmful. The finding is that a remediation market grows around the gap between what the institution certifies and what the student can do, and that the market's revenue depends on the gap persisting. The structural implication is direct and does not depend on the witness. Honest gapmapping would reduce the demand for the remediation market, because a student whose deficits were identified and addressed would need less supplementary tutoring. An institution whose revenue depends on supplementary remediation therefore has a financial interest in the gap remaining unmapped. This is the economic-surplus channel operating at the level of the individual classroom: the gap between certified and actual competence is itself a revenue stream, and closing it destroys the stream. 

The same logic explains why grade inflation is defended. Inflated grades preserve enrollment and reputation; honest assessment would expose the deficits. The documented retaliation record supports the inference. In Goudeau v. East Baton Rouge Parish School Board, the Fifth Circuit held that a teacher's speech about being ordered to change grades was protected and that transfer to a less desirable campus can be an adverse employment action; the principal had directed the teacher to change specific Fs to Ds. In Clovis Unified, a chemistry teacher received a formal Memorandum of Concern after resisting grade-inflation pressure and filed suit in December 2023, stating that her credential was at risk. In Henry County, Georgia, a fifth-grade teacher fired in 2017 for refusing to give grades below 60 percent had her case revived by the Georgia Supreme Court in 2022; she has been unable to find a teaching job since her firing. The Goudreau case is the clearest instance, because it supplies the artifact: the private book and the official book. The teacher who keeps two records has understood the aperture exactly. She knows that her institutional obligation runs to the official record and that her professional obligation runs to the composition, and she maintains both because they cannot be reconciled. 

The learned helplessness mechanism extends the moral-injury frame from teachers to students. The school's confinement apparatus — sit still for hours, attend to material selected by authority, curiosity treated as disruption, compliance rewarded over creativity — conditions the student population to be sortable by the aperture later. The student learns that his natural curiosity is a problem to be managed, not a capacity to be developed. He learns that his agency is a threat to the order of the 

classroom. He learns that compliance is rewarded and creativity is punished. He internalizes this as the structure of adult behavior, and he carries it into his career, where he then manages other people's agency the same way. The learned helplessness is not a side effect of mass education. It is the explicit product. The school is a training ground for the credentialed incompetent who will later fill the empty-room strategy, the fake-reference cartel, the LinkedIn potlatch, and the abstract-worshipping journal. The pattern across the retaliation record is consistent: the educator who insists on honest assessment is treated as the problem, and the treatment takes the form of soft neutralization rather than formal discipline — transfer, memorandum, reassignment — because formal discipline would create a visible dispute. The male-flight literature supplies the structural parallel. A study of male airline flight attendants found that they face job stress because of a female-dominated work environment and social prejudice, with job stress negatively affecting mental health and positively affecting turnover intention. The mechanism is not formal expulsion; it is the creation of conditions under which the actor removes themselves or is rendered functionally invisible. A reassignment to a role with no duties, while salary continues, is the institutional equivalent: the dissident is not fired — which would generate a record — but is removed from the scene while the appearance of normal employment is maintained. 

The four-stage blackmail architecture is the moral-liability channel in its operational form, and it appears in the space between the university and the corporation. The witness material describes a deliberate pattern: corrupt cohorts stall their contributions to group assignments, forcing the honest team members to wait, delay after delay, and then to rush to complete the work at the last moment. The corrupt members then introduce plagiarized material into the final submission. The plagiarism is not accidental. It is a tactic. The four stages are: Stage 1, STALL — delay contributions, force the honest member to rush, exhaust their capacity for verification. Stage 2, FRAME — introduce plagiarized material into the final submission at the last moment, exploiting the shared-authorship rule that puts all names on all submitted work. Stage 3, EXPLOIT — instructor complicity (relationships, apathy, administrative cost) validates the plagiarism on the institutional record; the instructor who overlooks it becomes an unwitting accomplice. Stage 4, ACTIVATE — the plagiarized submission is now a permanent, institutionally validated record of shared liability, which can be activated as blackmail if the honest member ever later threatens to expose the cohort's corporate fraud. The four-stage architecture has a specific aperture dependency at each stage. The stall exploits the deadline, which the aperture reads as the operative temporal fact. The frame exploits the shared-authorship rule, which the aperture reads as the operative authorship fact. The exploit exploits instructor discretion, which the aperture delegates to the frontline. The activate 

exploits the permanent record, which the aperture preserves as the operative institutional truth. 

The four-stage structure generalizes. The same architecture recurs in the for-profit college falsification schemes (§5.6), in which administrators delete a graduate's actual earnings and write new records from a salary website: stall (delay the audit), frame (manufacture the falsified record), exploit (regulatory complicity or inattention validates the new record), activate (the new record is then used to justify enrollment and extract aid). The architecture recurs in the two grade books (§4.3): the principal stalls (refuses to record the honest grades), frames (alters the official record), exploits (the audit accepts the official record), activates (the altered record is used to defend the institution's reputation). The four stages are the operational form of the moral-liability channel, and they appear wherever an institution's record can be captured by parties with interests divergent from the underlying condition the record is supposed to describe. The academic-integrity literature documents the mechanism in its group-project form. Group projects are particularly susceptible to integrity failures because of blurred authorship, unclear citation roles, and assumptions of shared responsibility. The documented failure modes are specific: copypasting shared research without attribution, misattributing individual contributions, submitting identical sections across teams, and over-relying on one member's writing. The deliberate variant the witness describes — the cohort that stalls, frames, exploits, and activates — is the rational response of a group that has understood that the institution reads the submission and not the contribution, which is the fit-to-sit doctrine applied to group work. The display signature recurs: the group invests its energy in the appearance of shared authorship rather than in the work, because the appearance is what the aperture reads. 

The moral-injury literature supplies the correct diagnosis, and the practitioner-facing version supplies the correct warning. The educator's distress is a response to being required to falsify a record, and labelling that distress a personal health problem is itself an operation of the aperture: it converts an institutional demand into an individual deficit, and it removes the institution from the audit trail that would otherwise record its role. What the university site establishes: first, the asymmetry is documented, not anecdotal — five verified cases across four US jurisdictions, three ending in settlements with public figures, one involving a DOJ finding. Second, the operative variable is documentary availability, not condition severity; Brown is decisive. Third, the pattern can be lawful; the Hong Kong case forecloses the compliance reading and specifies the remedy as a change in what must be registered rather than stricter enforcement of what already is. Fourth, the temporal architecture compounds the documentary one, and it operates in two registers — the claim that is filed late, and the claim that is never filed because the rule's 

existence suppressed it. Fifth, the gap between the official record and the underlying work is not a metaphor; it is two grade books, and the same structure recurs at institutional scale in the for-profit record. Sixth, resistance to honest assessment is structurally motivated; the shadoweducation literature establishes that remediation markets grow around the gap, and honest gap-mapping would reduce demand and expose institutional failure. Seventh, reform has come from outside — Hunter's policy was abandoned after litigation; Brown's revised under DOJ enforcement; Quinnipiac's changed by settlement; internal review did not produce these changes, consistent with Step 6 of the mechanism. 

What the university site does not establish: first, prevalence; seven cases over two decades establish that the pattern occurs and is legally cognizable; they do not establish frequency. Second, the paper does not directly document the physical-accommodation side; Goldfarb's counterfactual is testimony, not a policy document. Third, the cases are drawn heavily from US and Commonwealth institutions and the verified cases are all contested; institutions that accommodated without dispute generate no litigation and do not appear; the sample is selected on the outcome. Fourth, the paper cannot fully isolate legibility from stigma or risk-management cost; the response is that Goldfarb's counterfactual, Brown's denial in the face of confirming evidence, and the Hong Kong court's specification of what evidence would count all point to a mechanism operating beyond stigma. Fifth, the site does not establish intent; the mechanism is structural and interest-based, not malicious. Sixth, two of the seven cases are reported rather than verified, and their contribution is analytical rather than evidentiary; if they were removed, the site would lose the lawful-aperture argument and the two-book image, but the five verified cases and the policy language would remain sufficient to establish the asymmetry. 

##### **SECTION** 

### **§5 — Site 2: The Corporation** 

The corporate site reproduces the aperture with a different verification technology: the credential. Degrees, years of experience, titles, and references render a heterogeneous pool administratively comparable. The threshold character of the credential is well established. Collins argued that educational expansion produces stratified credentials rather than skills, and that inflation converts signals into entry requirements that no longer discriminate on competence. Contemporary evidence is consistent. Indeed reports that skills-based screening delivers roughly five times the predictive power of education requirements, that approximately 81 percent of job matches on its platform now occur through skills-based algorithms rather than credential matching, and that skills-first employers are approximately 7 percent less likely to lose high-performing employees. The OECD states 

that formal education cannot always capture the full range of skills individuals possess. The Stack Overflow 2024 survey reports 51 percent of professional developers did not learn to code through formal education. The ILO reports 58 percent informal employment globally. The implication is direct: if the credential is a threshold rather than a signal, the operative question for a candidate is not can you do the work but can you produce the document that clears the screen. Where the two diverge, the aperture selects for the document — and the document can be manufactured at scale and sold. 

Skills-first hiring is the master precedent for aperture widening, and it deserves elevation from the evidence-of-weak-credentials role it played in v2 to the master alternative role it occupies here. Skills-first hiring is the labor-market analogue of the raw-findings-first protocol in research (§11.3) and the engagement-performance alternative in pedagogy (§11.4). All three are instances of the same widening principle: verify by direct engagement with the material rather than by performance of engagement. Skills-first is the most empirically validated of the three. The Indeed data — 81 percent of matches via skills algorithms, 5x predictive power, 7 percent higher retention — establishes that widening is feasible at scale and does not produce the collapse in match quality that credentialist screening is meant to prevent. The direction is already established in practice. Skills-first also resolves the paradox-of-quotas angle (Angle 7): it may achieve diversity goals without rigid quotas, because it bypasses the credentialist aperture that quotas were intended to correct. Where skills-first has been adopted at scale, the diversity outcomes have not required quota mechanisms, because the aperture was widened rather than the selection criterion altered. This makes skills-first the master precedent for §11: the existence proof that widening at scale is feasible, and the design principle that unifies the four registers of alternative. 

The manufacture of references is structured, monetized, and criminalized. BeMyReference, a Reddit community, has approximately 56,000 members exchanging references with strangers. CareerExcuse supplies paid professional references, employment verification, and supporting company emails and websites, with plans from roughly $100 to $200 for one to three references across a roster of roughly 200 fictitious companies. A Standout CV analysis reports 25.4 percent of applicants admit lying about references, and 18.5 percent of that group have used a fake reference service, sometimes involving paid actors posing as employers. The enforcement record shows what happens when manufacture fails. Veronica Theriault used a fabricated referee to secure a $270,000-a-year chief information officer role, supported by a falsified degree and a LinkedIn photograph of a supermodel, and was sentenced to 25 months with a 12-month non-parole period. Andrew Flanagan was appointed group general manager of Myer at $400,000 and dismissed after falsifying his CV 

and arranging for friends to provide verbal references; the sentencing judge observed that enlisting dishonest friends to falsely vouch for you lifts the level of sophistication in your frauds. The conduct is independently criminalized: 18 U.S.C. § 1001, California Penal Code § 532, and Washington RCW 9A.60.070. 

Two features matter. First, the market exists because the reference is an operative fact, not because employers are gullible; the market supplies exactly the document the aperture reads. Second, the market is priced, so access is proportional to resources. The aperture does not merely reward those who possess the document; it rewards those who can buy it. The spillover cost is a general property of aperture capture: the manufactured signal degrades the value of the real signal for everyone who holds one. Where manufactured references circulate, suspicion attaches to the category rather than to the individual document. A candidate who presents a genuine reference from a genuine employer is evaluated against a background in which references are known to be purchasable, and the honest candidate bears a verification cost that the manufacturing candidate imposed. The point is analytical rather than empirical — it follows from the structure of the market — but it is the reason the reference market is not a victimless transaction between willing buyers and sellers. 

The kinship variant and total prestation. The witness material in Appendix C describes a variant in which student cohorts used businesses owned by peers or parents to issue references for roles in which no work was performed, with access obtained through reciprocity rather than cash. The paper does not present this as prevalence evidence. It presents it as an illustration of the form the market takes when participants lack capital to buy commercial references and possess instead the social capital to obtain them through reciprocity. The form is anthropologically familiar. Marcel Mauss's total prestation describes exchange in which goods, services, and recognition circulate together and create binding obligation. The kinship reference market is a total prestation — the reference is given, the subservience is returned, and the debt is never discharged because discharging it would end the relationship on which future references depend. This is why the transactions leave no paper trail: they are not cash transactions but social-debt transactions, and social debt is precisely what an audit cannot see. The aperture is calibrated to cash and contract, not to social debt, which is why the kinship market is invisible even where the cash market would be visible. The library cartel that meets to map highdemand niches and conspire to fabricate experience is Marcel Mauss's total prestation operating inside a modern university. The transactions are invisible to the audit not by accident but by structural design. 

This has implications for the conspiracy objection (§10.5). The kinship market requires no coordination beyond reciprocity, which is the structural 

reason it cannot be prosecuted as conspiracy. A conspiracy requires agreement; the kinship market requires only that each participant benefit from the ongoing relationship, and the benefit is symmetric — each gives references and each receives them. The structure is self-maintaining without any central coordinator, because the social debt that holds it together is exactly the asset that would be destroyed by disclosure. A participant who exposed the market would forfeit the social capital that gave him access to it, and so the market is protected by the same mechanism that creates it. This is why the corrupt informal economy is structurally harder to detect than the honest informal economy, which leaves wage traces (gig platforms, crowdwork platforms) and contract traces (1099 forms, payment processor records). The honest informal economy is visible to an audit that knows where to look; the corrupt informal economy is invisible to any audit, because the medium of exchange is itself the audit-blind spot. 

Ghost postings: a substantial share of postings are not backed by intent to hire. MyPerfectResume surveyed 753 U.S. recruiters and found approximately eight in ten reporting their company posts jobs already filled or non-existent. Resume Builder surveyed 649 hiring managers and found 40 percent had posted a fake listing in the past year. Clarify Capital found nearly one in three employers admit posting with no current intent to hire. The Columbia Law Review analysis reports that the hires-per-posting ratio has approximately halved since 2019, from roughly eight per ten to roughly four, and places the technology sector's ghost rate at around 48 percent. Ghost postings perform several functions: sustaining an appearance of growth to investors; building pipelines without commitment; supporting claims of labor scarcity useful in lobbying, visa allocation, and explanations of underperformance to shareholders. The common structure is that the posting is a signal whose function is not to be filled but to be seen. 

The claim must be stated at the correct level. The paper does not claim ghost postings are created in order to be filled by fabricated-reference candidates. What the verified record establishes is the conjunction of two independently documented facts: a large supply of postings not intended to be filled, and a large priced market in manufactured references. The theory predicts that where allocation runs through a document and the document can be manufactured, manufacture will occur. Coexistence is consistent with the prediction; it does not establish coordination. Plagiarism traps and preemptive liability: the four-stage architecture (§4.6) belongs in this section because it is the same mechanism operating in the space between the university and the firm. The structure is recognizable as a control tactic. By ensuring all participants have dirty hands, the group eliminates the risk of internal reporting. The instructor who overlooks the plagiarism becomes an unwitting accomplice, validating the record on which the constraint depends. The paper does not claim this is common; it claims it is the 

rational response of a group that has understood that the institution reads the submission and not the contribution. 

H-1B extraction and the fissured workplace. In the HCL Technologies matter, a False Claims Act whistleblower alleged the firm systematically underpaid its H-1B workers by approximately $95 million annually as a core competitive strategy, with workers placed at Disney, Google, FedEx, and Boeing. The Economic Policy Institute characterized this as standard industry practice, enabled by a Department of Labor loophole permitting firms to disclaim responsibility when the worker is hired through a contractor. At Southern California Edison, approximately 500 IT employees were laid off in 2015 and required to train their lower-paid H-1B replacements as a condition of severance, with non-disparagement clauses used to silence them. Weil's fissured-workplace framework supplies the structural account: workers performing identical tasks receive radically different compensation depending on their contractual relationship to the lead firm rather than their skill. The fissure is not a market failure but a legal architecture that lets the lead firm preserve brand value while displacing employment risk. The H-1B cases are the fissure in pure form: the visa category, not the work, is the operative fact. For the theory, the fissure is the institutional habitat of credential manufacture. The shell company, the parental business, and the contractor intermediary all occupy the space where oversight is thin and the capacity to issue credentials is intact. The fissure explains why the reference market can operate at scale: it provides entities that can verify employment without being asked to demonstrate it occurred. 

The feedback loop: for-profit colleges and surplus extraction. The corporate and university sites are not independent. They are connected by a loop in which manufactured labor-market demand justifies educational expansion, and expansion supplies the credentials the labor market's aperture reads. At ATI Enterprises, an independent accounting firm found 90 percent of programs in Texas had significantly overreported job placement rates, with 63 percent below the required 60 percent threshold. At Education Management Corporation, a career-services advisor testified before the U.S. Senate that graduates needed only one day of work to be counted as placed, and that advisors manipulated student information to ensure placement could be recorded, constructing new records from salarywebsite averages when actual earnings were too low. At Kaplan University, a whistleblower alleged a scheme to defraud the federal government of more than $4 billion by enrolling unqualified students, inflating grades, falsifying accreditation documents, and paying the institution's own employees to enroll to satisfy a revenue-composition requirement. At ITT Educational Services, a director observed staff changing failing scores to passing scores on placement tests and generating inaccurate placement figures. The University of Phoenix paid $9.8 million to resolve recruiter- 

compensation allegations in 2004 and later faced a False Claims Act suit alleging similar conduct. At Corinthian Colleges, an internal investigation found that administrators at a single Texas campus falsified the employment records of 288 graduates over four years. 

The loop has four steps. First, the corporation's aperture reads credentials and placement statistics as signals. Second, institutions that cannot produce those signals honestly have an incentive to manufacture them, because funding and accreditation depend on the signals. Third, manufactured signals justify further enrollment, because prospective students read placement rates as evidence of demand. Fourth, when graduates fail to find the promised employment, the outcome is attributed to market dynamics. The EDMC testimony is worth pausing on, because it is the institutional form of the two grade books. An advisor who deletes a graduate's actual earnings and writes a new record from an average salary website is doing what the Louisiana teacher's principal did: replacing the composition with an aggregate that the audit will accept. The scale differs. The structure does not. The surplus channel, and its direction: administrative spending is a primary driver of tuition inflation. Between 2010 and 2018, student-services spending at public institutions grew at 2.32 percent annually, nearly triple instructional spending growth; at private institutions, every dollar of student-services spending was associated with $1.12 in tuition increases. The Progressive Policy Institute finds that only 40 cents of every federal aid dollar actually reduces student prices; the remaining 60 cents is absorbed institutionally for salaries and non-instructional expenses. A BMC Public Health study finds that higher levels of student debt are associated with lower community well-being, including declines in homeownership and entrepreneurship and increases in poor health behaviors. 

The economics complete the loop. The credential's threshold function allows the institution to charge for issuance independently of delivered value; administrative absorption converts tuition and aid into noninstructional expenditure; the residual risk is transferred to individual borrowers; and the community-level consequences of concentrated debt appear as a decline in entrepreneurship — that is, as a decline in the capacity to do exactly what the credential was supposed to certify. The Corinthian advertising record adds the element the aggregate data cannot supply: direction. The corpus reports that Corinthian spent over $600,000 on two weeks of advertising on BET targeting the Black community, in the context of a scheme whose placement rates were being falsified. The figure is reported rather than independently verified here, and it is used only for the point the verified aggregate data cannot make. That point is that surplus extraction need not be diffuse. It can be directed: an institution can spend heavily to recruit a specific population while knowing, in its own internal records, that the promised outcome will not be delivered. The 

aggregate data show that surplus moves; the advertising figure shows that someone chose where it should move from. The economic-surplus channel therefore moves value from students and the public purse to institutional administration, and the aperture that reads credentials and placement statistics cannot see the movement because it is registered only in budget categories the audit does not examine — and, where records can be written, because the movement can be recorded as its opposite. 

The honest and the corrupt informal economy. The corporate site clarifies a distinction the theory requires. There are two informal economies in the shadow of the credential, and they are structurally different. The honest economy is documented in the labor statistics: the ILO's 58 percent informal-employment figure; the Fairwork Italia finding of approximately 600,000 platform workers, more than three-quarters of them men aged 30 to 49, with 36 percent in food delivery, 14 percent in distribution, and 35 percent in online crowdwork such as IT and translation, 57.6 percent selfemployed and 31 percent without a written contract; and the DollarSprout finding that 72.4 percent of U.S. men and 66.8 percent of women report a side gig. Gray and Suri's account of ghost work supplies the frame: this labor is not marginal but is kept deliberately concealed, because the visibility of the platform depends on the invisibility of the worker. The corrupt economy is the reference market of §5.2. It is also productive — of documents — and also invisible to the formal audit, but its output is the manufactured signal rather than the delivered skill. 

The two are not morally equivalent and the paper does not treat them as such. But they are structurally homologous in one respect that matters: both are responses to an aperture that allocates by credential, and both operate in the space the aperture cannot see. The honest response is exit into unmeasured productive work; the corrupt response is entry into the manufacture of measured but unproductive signals. The aperture cannot distinguish them because it reads the document, not the condition. The Greater Male Variability Hypothesis sharpens the honest side: the most skilled exiters are also the least visible, because their exits are read as outliers rather than as the population the theory predicts. The aggregate reports no loss because the loss is in the tail, and the tail is what the aggregate averages away. Which response an actor takes is not random. It depends on resources: capital to purchase a manufactured reference, social capital to obtain one through reciprocity, or the absence of both, in which case exit is the only available option. The aperture therefore does not merely sort the documented from the undocumented; it sorts along a line that was drawn by prior advantage. This is the labor channel's most uncomfortable implication: the honest informal economy is not a free choice, and the corrupt formal economy is not equally available to everyone. The two directions of reallocation are, in this respect, the same reallocation 

viewed from different ends — real value out, fake value in, and both movements conditioned by who could afford which. 

What this site establishes: first, the credential threshold is empirically weak as a predictor of competence. Second, reference manufacture is documented, priced, and criminalized, and it imposes a verification cost on honest holders of genuine references. Third, the demand side is also manufactured. Fourth, the aperture operates at institutional scale through fissured employment and through the for-profit feedback loop, and the economic-surplus channel is documented independently through administrative-bloat and aid-absorption data, with the advertising record supplying direction. Fifth, the official record can be rewritten, and where it is rewritten the aggregate and the composition diverge — the institutional form of the two grade books. Sixth, honest and corrupt informal economies are structurally homologous responses to the same aperture, and the choice between them is constrained by prior resources. What this site does not establish: first, the prevalence of fabricated references among actual hires; survey data report self-reported applicant behavior, prosecutions are selected for detection, commercial services document supply rather than uptake. Second, coordination between ghost postings and manufactured references. Third, the lobbying mechanism described in the source files; the H-1B cases establish underpayment and displacement; the EPI characterization supports standard practice; it does not establish the lobbying chain. Fourth, generalization from the for-profit sector to elite institutions or mainstream corporate hiring. Fifth, that a skills-based aperture would eliminate fraud; it would change what must be manufactured; it would create its own verification problems. Sixth, the Corinthian advertising figure is reported rather than verified, and the direction argument does not depend on the specific sum.

### **§6 — Site 3: Knowledge Production** 

The publishing site satisfies all three conditions — heterogeneous readership, documentary verification, high stakes — with a different technology: the abstract. The abstract is the standardized document the system reads. Search platforms display it, indexing services record it, citation managers surface it, readers encounter it before anything else. In Power's terms it is the ritual of verification: a compact, auditable object standing in for the work. The full text sits behind it, the dataset behind the full text, the code behind the dataset. Each layer is a simplification, and each simplification is what makes the next layer processable. The theory predicts what follows. If the aperture is calibrated to the abstract, authors have an incentive to make it persuasive — to frame the finding through a lens that maximizes citation and perceived importance — and the reader's 

first encounter is an interpretation rather than an observation. The claim is architectural, not empirical: the abstract is a standardized filter, the filter precedes the evidence, and the filter is authored by the party whose interest is in the interpretation. 

The socialization mechanism, illustrated. The clearest available illustration of what this architecture does to new practitioners is the transmission of a canonical number. A student entering the study of memory encounters the claim that working-memory capacity is roughly seven items, learns it as a finding, and designs experiments around it. The number becomes an axiom before the student has seen the conditions under which it was established. When data later show capacity varying substantially with material type and task instruction, the variation is read as an interesting complication rather than as evidence that the original number was condition-specific. The mechanism is not that the student is credulous; it is that the framing arrived before the data and organized what counted as a finding. This is legibility bias operating inside the profession: what the field can recognize is what its categories can accommodate, and the categories arrive first. 

The platform architecture: the full text is frequently behind a paywall while the abstract is displayed. The architecture is not incidental. Library discovery systems and union catalogues are designed as teasers: the first page of a record displays metadata and abstract, and access to the full text is gated behind an institutional login. The design teaches the reader, through repetition, that the interpretation is public and the evidence is privileged. This is a stronger claim than that full texts are paywalled, because it concerns what the architecture trains. A reader who has learned to work from abstracts has learned to work from the layer the aperture reads, and has learned it as a matter of habit rather than constraint. The sorting that results is homologous to §4 and §5: in each site there is a cheap, standardized document the aperture reads and an expensive, unstructured artifact it cannot process. The doctor's note is cheap; the narrative of coercive control is expensive. The reference is cheap; the demonstrated skill is expensive. The abstract is cheap; the replication dataset is expensive. The aperture does not choose the cheap document because it is epistemically superior; it chooses it because it is the form in which the institution can satisfy its audit. 

Post-hoc review, the self-report analogy, and the LARP diagnosis. Peer review evaluates a completed manuscript. It does not ordinarily participate in design, calibration, or measurement specification. By the time reviewers see the work, the interpretive frame is set, the data collected, the analysis conducted. Review asks whether the narrative is coherent and the conclusions defensible; it does not ordinarily ask whether the instrument measured what it claimed, or whether a plausible bias was evaluated before 

collection rather than acknowledged after. The structural observation — review occurs after the frame is set — is verifiable by inspection of workflow. The stronger claim, that post-hoc review systematically fails to detect measurement bias, is not established by the corpus and is not asserted. 

The LARP diagnosis, restored. The source corpus uses the term larp to describe peer-reviewed science as performative roleplay, and the term is sharper than the analogy it softens to. The performative element is structurally necessary, not incidental, because careers depend on publication metrics. The survey respondent who answers as they believe the researcher wishes and the author who frames the finding as they believe reviewers will reward are performing the same optimization against the same kind of audience. The difference is that the survey literature has a well-developed account of social-desirability bias and the publishing literature does not. The LARP framing is not cynicism; it is the structural prediction of the theory under audit pressure. The display signature (§3.3) recurs in its purest form: the researcher invests in the appearance of competence-signaling findings rather than in the underlying competence, because the appearance is what the aperture reads. 

The deterministic conversion. A distinct and sharper point is available. Once a finding is published in a reputable venue, it is treated as settled rather than probabilistic. Science, as a method, holds findings as provisional and revisable; publication practice converts them into axioms. New researchers treat them as starting assumptions; subsequent work refines within the frame rather than testing it; and the frame's authority is reinforced by the citation record. The consequence is that the cost of revisiting a published interpretation rises over time, because the interpretation has accumulated the weight of the literature that built on it. The deterministic conversion compounds the LARP, because the performance becomes the axiomatic starting point for the next generation of researchers. The performance of competence in the first generation becomes the inherited framework of the second generation, and the second generation's LARP is built on top of the first's, with no point in the chain at which the underlying raw findings are interrogated directly. 

This is the publishing site's version of the fit-to-sit doctrine. In both cases, submission is treated as a declaration that the underlying work is sound, and the audit arrives too late to interrogate the declaration. The difference is that the university's doctrine is codified policy while publishing's is a workflow convention; the similarity of form is what the cross-site claim rests on. The deterministic conversion also explains why the interest account (Step 6, §3.5) bites hardest here. A senior researcher's authority is bound to the frameworks the field recognizes. A framework that has been converted into settled fact is a framework no one need revisit — 

and the senior researcher's position depends on it not being revisited. Widening the aperture at this site means reopening settled questions, which is to say it means redistributing authority. This is why reform in publishing tends to arrive as infrastructure — pre-registration, data-availability mandates, open access — rather than as internal revision, and why the courage literature's finding applies: what counts is a changed record, not a changed attitude. 

The research-participation problem. Where universities require students to accumulate research-participation credit, the requirement creates a captive sample. The students who participate are those who need the credit; they participate under pressure; and the quality of participation is shaped by the incentive. Two claims should be distinguished. The weaker is the behavioral signature described in §3.3: effort is directed toward appearing compliant rather than toward the task, so that the energy is spent on the display rather than the participation. The stronger is a claim about the composition of the evidence base: if universities use students' credential need as a disproportionate share of the sourcing for research, the resulting sample is narrow in demographic terms and coerced in motivational terms, and the bias propagates into the findings the publishing aperture then reads. The corpus contains the author's direct observation of the pattern over an extended period; it contains no systematic study. The paper therefore states the weaker claim as a theoretical prediction, notes the stronger as a reported observation requiring verification, and defers the witness to Appendix C. 

What this site establishes: first, the architecture is verifiable — the abstract is the standardized document, the evidence is behind it. Second, the architecture satisfies the three conditions, so the theoretical prediction is strong. Third, the temporal architecture reproduces the fit-to-sit form, and publication converts a probability statement into a settled fact — a conversion whose cost is borne by whoever later needs to reopen the question. What this site does not establish: first, the section is illustrative rather than demonstrative; the university site rests on five verified cases, two reported cases, and three verified policy samples; the corporate site on survey data, litigation, and statutory text; publishing rests on architectural observation and limited sourcing; the theoretical prediction is strong; the evidential confidence is moderate-to-weak, and §3.7 records the asymmetry. Second, the paper does not establish that abstracts misrepresent findings at any measurable rate. Third, the paper does not establish that peer review fails to detect bias; it establishes that review arrives after the frame is set. Fourth, the section does not engage the substantial literature on peerreview reliability, open science, or pre-registration, and does not claim to supersede it; its contribution is narrower: the publishing site exhibits the same aperture logic, which demonstrates the mechanism is not confined to law, medicine, or employment. Fifth, the raw-findings-first protocol 

proposed in the source corpus is untested and appears only in Appendix E, marked as such. 

##### **SECTION** 

### **§7 — Site 4: Mass Schooling as Population Conditioning** 

The fourth site is unlike the other three. It is not a fourth instance of the same failure mode. It is the developmental precondition that produces the population the other three sites sort. The school is where the population is conditioned to be sortable by an aperture — to accept credential as proxy for competence, compliance as proxy for engagement, performance as proxy for understanding. Without this conditioning, the university, the corporation, and the journal would face a population that did not internalize the aperture as natural, and the aperture's operation would be visible as the contingent design choice it is. With the conditioning, the aperture becomes the water the population swims in, and the reallocation it produces is read as the natural distribution of talent and effort rather than as the output of a specific institutional arrangement. 

Mass schooling conditions the population through three mechanisms that operate in sequence and reinforce each other. The first is performance over engagement. The school asks the student to sit still for hours, to attend to material selected by authority, to refrain from creative deviation, to be confined indoors instead of spending most of his time outside, to accept that his natural curiosity is a disruption to be managed rather than a capacity to be developed. The student who complies is rewarded; the student who creates is punished. The student who can best perform the role of student — sit, attend, comply, perform on test — succeeds, regardless of whether he has actually understood anything. The student who actually understands but cannot perform fails. The system sorts the performer upward and the understander out, and reports the sorting as meritocratic. The display signature (§3.3) appears here in its developmental form: the student learns to invest energy in the appearance of engagement rather than in the engagement itself, because the appearance is what the aperture reads. The school is the first site at which the rational response to the aperture is taught, and it is taught before the student has any framework for recognizing it as a response rather than as the structure of reality. 

The second mechanism is learned helplessness, in Seligman's classical sense. The classical experiments describe how organisms that learn their actions cannot affect outcomes cease to act, even when escape becomes available. The translation to the classroom is direct. A student whose curiosity is treated as disruption, whose compliance is rewarded over creativity, and whose agency is managed rather than cultivated learns that 

agency is not available inside the institution. The student internalizes this as the structure of adult behavior and carries it into the career, where he then manages other people's agency the same way. Learned helplessness is not a side effect of mass education. It is the explicit product. The school is a training ground for the credentialed incompetent who will later fill the empty-room strategy (§4.6), the fake-reference cartel (§5.2), the LinkedIn potlatch, and the abstract-worshipping journal (§6). The population that emerges from the school is the population that has been trained to accept the aperture as the natural order, and to perform rather than to engage, and to confuse the performance with the underlying reality. 

The third mechanism is the extinguishing of the daemon, in Plato's sense of the innate creative capacity. The daemon is the unique inner spark of creative insight that cannot be taught but must emerge from engagement. The genie is the capacity to create something new. The divine spark is the innate wisdom and pattern recognition, the ability to see connections others miss. When the paper says that schools extinguish these qualities through confinement rather than cultivation, it is describing a system that treats creativity as dangerous or disruptive rather than as essential for solving real problems. The school does not merely fail to cultivate the daemon. It actively trains it out, through years of compliance reinforcement, through the punishment of deviation, through the reward of performance over engagement. The graduate who emerges is not the uneducated version of the person who entered. He is the de-educated version — the person whose native capacity for direct engagement with reality has been systematically replaced with a capacity for performance of engagement that he can no longer distinguish from the real thing. 

The biological mismatch argument, reframed. The source corpus contains an inclination to explain institutional phenomena through physical or neurological frameworks. The caution against this is real: a neurological model does not explain why a dean reads a doctor's note rather than a narrative of coercive control; an audit-pressure account does. Where a physical finding specifies a mechanism bearing on institutional design, the paper uses it with the pathway stated. The biological mismatch argument is one such case. The claim is institutional, not neurological. The claim is about the timing of credential sorting relative to the development of the capacity the credential claims to measure. Prefrontal development continues into the mid-twenties and, on some accounts, into the midthirties. The credential is therefore issued at age eighteen or twenty-two, at a developmental moment when the capacity it claims to measure (judgment, long-term planning, integrative understanding) is not yet mature. This is an aperture-timing claim: the aperture mistimes measurement, issuing the credential before the capacity matures. The institutional point is that the credential sorts at the moment the population is most developmentally vulnerable to being sorted wrong, and the population so sorted then carries 

the credential forward into the university and the corporation and the journal, where the aperture reads the credential as a signal of capacity that was not yet present at the moment of issuance. 

This is the developmental layer of the timing claim in the measurement corollary (§3.6). The aperture does not merely conceal composition and tail; it mistimes measurement. The school issues the credential at the developmental moment when the population is most legible (the student who can sit still and take the test) and least developed in the capacity the credential is supposed to certify. The result is that the credential-certified population is systematically skewed toward those who matured early in the legible dimensions (compliance, test-taking, performance) and systematically skewed away from those who matured early in the illegible dimensions (creative insight, integrative judgment, daemon). The aperture thus produces a population whose legible capacities were mature at the moment of sorting and whose illegible capacities were not, and the population carries this skew forward into every subsequent site. 

Hoarding as rational adaptation. The school produces mass people in poor health, with poor skills, ending at age twenty already rather late biologically and historically, with only a miserly salary. They then enter a labor market in which they must compete for jobs given by scarcity-creating hoarders. The competitive frame conditions the student to view other people as competitors rather than collaborators, to view resources as zerosum rather than expandable, to view his own success as requiring the failure of others. The credentialist framework then rewards those who can most effectively manufacture the appearance of competence rather than those who have actually developed competence. The hoarder is the rational adaptation to this framework: he who accumulates credentials, references, network connections, and visible signals of achievement accumulates the tokens that the framework rewards, regardless of whether those tokens correspond to underlying capability. The hoarder is not a moral failure. He is the optimal strategy within the framework the school has constructed. 

The shadow education feedback. The shadow-education literature (Asian Development Bank; Bray) documents that private supplementary tutoring maintains and exacerbates educational inequality because prosperous families purchase greater quantities and better qualities of tutoring. In India, 25.9 percent of school dropouts in Delhi slums cited inability to afford private tutoring as the reason for withdrawal; parents and students believed that attending school without tutoring yielded no positive result. The finding is not that tutoring is harmful. The finding is that a remediation market grows around the gap between what the institution certifies and what the student can do, and that the market's revenue depends on the gap persisting. Honest gap-mapping would reduce the demand for the remediation market, because a student whose deficits were identified and 

addressed would need less supplementary tutoring. An institution whose revenue depends on supplementary remediation therefore has a financial interest in the gap remaining unmapped. The shadow education feedback loop is the developmental form of the economic-surplus channel: surplus is extracted from families who pay for tutoring to compensate for the school's failure to deliver, and the extraction is invisible to the school's audit because the school's audit reads grades and attendance rather than the underlying competence. 

What this site establishes. First, the school is the developmental precondition for the other three sites, not a fourth instance of the same failure mode. Second, the school's failure signature is conditioning — the manufacture of a population that has internalized the aperture as natural — distinct from the detection failure of the university, the authentication failure of the corporation, and the sequencing failure of publishing. Third, the school explains the cross-generational reproduction of the aperture: each generation enters the university, the corporation, and the journal already conditioned to accept the aperture, and the conditioning is invisible to the conditioned population because it is the water they have been swimming in since age five. Fourth, the biological mismatch argument, reframed as an aperture-timing claim, specifies a developmental mechanism by which the credential-sorting moment is mistimed relative to the capacity it claims to measure. Fifth, the hoarding response emerges as the rational adaptation to the manufactured scarcity the school produces, and the hoarder is the optimal strategy within the framework the school has constructed. 

What this site does not establish. First, no prevalence data on learned helplessness outcomes are claimed; the mechanism is theoretical, supported by the Seligman tradition and the practitioner-facing literature on moral injury. Second, the Plato frame is interpretive, not empirical; the daemon is a hermeneutic resource for naming what the school extinguishes, not a measurable outcome variable. Third, the biological-mismatch pathway requires direct sourcing; the prefrontal development finding is widely reported but the specific source for the timing claim (mid-twenties to midthirties) must be cited before submission, and the institutional argument does not depend on the precise dating. Fourth, the school site is supported by architectural observation, the shadow-education literature, and the learned-helplessness tradition; it does not have the case-record weight of the university site or the survey-and-litigation weight of the corporate site. Fifth, the engagement-performance alternative proposed in §11.4 is the design-principle response to this site, and it is marked untested.

### **§8 — Cross-Site Comparison** 

The four sites share a common mechanism. The demand differs by site because the principal differs — dean, hiring committee, reviewer, school board — but the demand is structurally the same: make your decision auditable. The cheapest way to make a decision auditable is to route it through a document the principal can inspect without investigating the case. The document is not a lie; it is a proxy selected for auditability rather than validity. At the university site, the accountability demand is consistency and non-arbitrariness of mercy decisions; the standardized document is the medical note, accommodation form, deadline; the condition it cannot register is coercive control, trauma, impaired capacity to selfadvocate. At the corporate site, the accountability demand is merit-based, defensible selection; the standardized document is the degree, years, reference, posted requirement; the condition it cannot register is demonstrated skill without credential, honest informal experience. At the publishing site, the accountability demand is rigor and importance; the standardized document is the abstract, methods summary, citation record; the condition it cannot register is measurement quality, uninterpreted findings, replication. At the school site, the accountability demand is developmental readiness for downstream sorting; the standardized document is the grade, test score, attendance record, behavioral compliance report; the condition it cannot register is creative engagement, daemon, the capacity for direct engagement with material that no standardized instrument can capture. 

Four verification technologies, four failure signatures. The university's technology is medical-bureaucratic, calibrated to clinical legibility; its characteristic failure is the routing of psychological crisis into conduct or liability categories — a failure of detection. The corporation's technology is credential-based, calibrated to pedigree legibility; its characteristic failure is the manufacture of the document — a failure of authentication. Publishing's technology is summary-based, calibrated to narrative legibility; its characteristic failure is that interpretation precedes and conditions the encounter with evidence — a failure of sequencing. The school's technology is performance-record, calibrated to compliance legibility; its characteristic failure is the manufacture of a population that has internalized the aperture as natural — a failure of conditioning. The paper's claim is that the four signatures are variants of one pattern: the institution reads the document because the document is auditable, and the condition diverges from the document in a systematic direction — toward the interests of the party who can produce documents and away from the party who cannot. 

The phase cycle. The channels are not simultaneous. They are ordered, and the order is what allows the arrangement to reproduce itself. The cycle begins with the school, which conditions the population. The school supplies 

the population the university then sorts; the population arrives already trained to accept the aperture as natural and to perform rather than to engage. The university then issues the credential. The credential creates the transition market, in which access is obtained through manufactured or brokered signals. The transition market supplies the corporation, which reads the credential as a competence proxy and posts requirements that only manufactured signals can meet. The corporate demand signal returns to the university as justification for enrollment. And at every stage the neutralization phase suppresses the witnesses who could interrupt the sequence. The arrangement is therefore not a set of independent institutional failures. It is a cycle, and each phase supplies the conditions of the next, beginning with the school that conditions the population that fills every subsequent phase. 

The micro-mechanism of reproduction. The cycle is not maintained by coercion; it is maintained by incentive. An actor who is honest today and is filtered out by the aperture faces a choice between exit and manufacture. Exit is available and is what most actors choose — which is why the honest informal economy is large. But exit removes the honest actor from the pool the aperture can see, and manufacture inserts the dishonest actor into it. Over iterations, the visible pool becomes more manufactured and less honest, not because actors are corrupting but because the aperture has been selecting. This is the dynamic consequence stated in §3.5, and it is the reason the cycle does not self-correct: the aperture's own operation degrades the signal it reads, and the degradation is invisible to the aperture because the aperture measures the signal's presence rather than its provenance. 

Common failure mode, not common cause. The four sites share a failure mode, not a cause. The cause of each aperture is local: the university's by ADA compliance, liability management, and academic governance; the corporation's by labor law, credential norms, and fissured employment; publishing's by the economics of access and review convention; the school's by developmental psychology, credentialist demand from downstream institutions, and the political economy of mass education. The paper does not claim these are the same. What it claims is that given the local causes, the aperture takes a characteristic form and produces a characteristic reallocation. This is why §3.7 separates theoretical prediction from evidential confidence, and why §3.6 states the measurement corollary: aggregate outcome measures conceal compositional reallocation, so a test of the theory must measure who clears the aperture, not merely how many. 

The two grade books are the corollary's emblem, and they recur at every scale. A teacher's private book against the administration's official one. A graduate's actual earnings against the record written from a salary website. A class of students who submitted work against a fit-to-sit declaration that 

no accommodation was needed. A population of schoolchildren whose creative capacities were extinguished against a transcript that records only grades and attendance. In each case, the aggregate is available to the audit and the composition is not; and where the record can be rewritten, the composition is not merely unavailable but is replaced by a false aggregate. This is why the theory predicts that aggregate measures will be the last thing to move: the aperture does not merely fail to register composition, it registers a substitution for it. 

The meta-application: the theory applies to itself. The original source critique was specifically a critique of gender quota research's no-loss claim — the assertion that quotas do not reduce male engagement or productivity. The claim is not, strictly speaking, empirically false. It is epistemologically incomplete: a conclusion that is true only within the narrow measurement boundaries that researchers have chosen. The quota researchers' aperture was the formal labor market — formal employment, formal wages, formal tournament performance. They reported no loss because they measured no loss. The ILO's finding that 58 percent of global workers are informally employed, with men at 60 percent, describes the population their aperture could not register. The honest informal economy — the gig workers, the open-source developers, the self-taught programmers, the community organizers — is invisible to the formal-employment aperture. The no-loss claim is legibility bias operating inside the research itself. 

This is not a complaint about quota research. It is the theory's strongest demonstration of its own reach. The same aperture logic that produces the asymmetry at the university and the corporation and the journal and the school produces the no-loss claim inside the research that studies the asymmetry. The theory's claim that aggregate outcome measures conceal compositional reallocation applies to the aggregate outcome measures that quota research reports: the formal employment rate, the formal wage gap, the formal tournament outcome. These aggregates are insensitive to the composition of who remains in the formal market and who exits into the informal market. They report no loss because they cannot register the exit. And the theory applies to this paper as well. The paper has its own aperture: the verified / reported / witnessed evidentiary registers. The paper's own aggregate — eleven cases, seventeen angles, five channels, four sites — conceals its own composition: which cases are verified, which angles are diagnostic, which channels are site-derived. The paper's response to this self-application is not to deny it but to make the registers visible (§11.6). The wager is that making one's own aperture visible is the precondition for changing it — and that this is the discipline the paper demands of institutions and therefore must demand of itself. 

The distinction between common failure mode and common cause also disposes of the conspiracy objection. A conspiracy requires coordination; 

the mechanism requires only that institutions responding to different accountability demands with different documentary technologies converge on the same audit-driven rationality. Convergence of this kind is the ordinary output of institutional isomorphism under audit pressure (DiMaggio and Powell 1983). The paper's claim is structural, and its modesty is its strength. The counter-configurations in the lower rows of §3.7 are the falsification conditions in comparative form: if small-seminar grading and craft apprenticeship showed the same pattern as the highstakes documentary sites, the mechanism would not be doing the explanatory work.

### **§9 — Cases That Don't Fit** 

The Finnish teacher quota: selection can be corrected. Schaede and Mankki (2026), in the American Economic Review, find that the Finnish teacher quota advantaged academically lower-scoring male applicants, that its removal cut the share of men among new teachers by half, that pupils exposed to male quota teachers transition more smoothly to postcompulsory education and attain more, and that the quota improved the allocation of talent by mending imperfections in the unconstrained selection process. This is a genuine counter-example to the strong pessimist claim that formal selection is irredeemably captured. It is not a counter-example to the theory. Read through the framework, the quota is an instance of aperture widening: the unconstrained process read academic scores; the quota added a second criterion; the second criterion registered something the first could not. The result was not a distortion of merit but a more accurate rendering of it. The case imposes discipline on the paper's rhetoric. It does not license the inference that quotas are generally corrective. A quota applied to a well-functioning process may degrade allocation; a quota applied to a demonstrably defective process may improve it. The variable is whether the defect is real and identified. Through the diagnostic lens of the seventeen angles (§3.8), the Finnish case resolves Angle 7 (the paradox of quotas) and Angle 14 (the meritocracy assumption): the quota worked because the pre-quota merit measure was demonstrably defective, and the quota was an aperture-widening device rather than a distortion of an accurate measure. 

Brown after the DOJ: what courage actually consists of. Brown paid $684,000 and revised its policies under ADA Title III. The revision is analytically important: the institution did not merely pay; it changed what it would record and how. The change was compelled, and that is the point. The courage literature specifies why the revision counts and a public statement would not. Courage attenuates the harm of betrayal only when the institution acts with transparency and accountability — that is, when it changes the record. Brown's revision changed the record: it altered the conditions under which readmission decisions would be made and documented. The institution did not become more compassionate in any way the paper can measure. It became auditable in a different way, and that is what the literature predicts would help. Three lessons follow. First, legibility bias is not inevitable. Second, the mechanism of change was legal, not moral: the institution did not discover the injustice through internal review but through enforcement — consistent with Step 6 of the mechanism. Third, the case supplies the model for §11: widening is an engineering problem — changing what the institution must demonstrate and record — rather than a problem of persuading administrators to be 

kinder. Kinder is not a specification. Record this, in this form, and show me the file is. 

It also constrains the claim. Brown's revision addressed one aperture (readmission after medical leave) and did not, so far as the record shows, address the notification-window or fit-to-sit mechanisms. Aperture widening is local; it proceeds one verification technology at a time. Brown is also the parallel instance to skills-first hiring (§5.1): both are aperture-widenings, one in university mercy, one in corporate screening. The two cases together establish that widening is feasible across the arrangement, not only within one site. 

The counter-configurations: where the pattern should not appear. Smallseminar grading lacks Condition 2. The instructor does not read a document standing in for the student; the instructor reads the student. The theory predicts allocation should track demonstrated capacity more closely than in the extenuating-circumstances regime. This does not mean seminar grading is unbiased; it means it should not exhibit legibility bias, because there is no documentary filter to produce it. Craft apprenticeship lacks Condition 2 in the same way. Advancement is determined by demonstrated work product; the master verifies competence by observing the work, not by reading a certificate. The theory predicts the characteristic failure mode of apprenticeship will be other than legibility bias — favoritism, idiosyncratic standards, or the master's limited observational capacity. The craft model is the structural basis for the raw-findings proposal in Appendix E: if practitioners learn by direct engagement with the material before encountering the field's packaged interpretation, the aperture is bypassed rather than widened. The craft model is also the structural analogue of the engagement-performance alternative in §11.4: both verify by direct engagement with material rather than by performance of engagement. Lowstakes forms and homogeneous compliance checks lack Conditions 3 and 1 respectively. They may exhibit the documentary preference, but reallocation is inconsequential or the population is not heterogeneous enough for divergence to arise. These are the theory's comparative cases. If seminar grading and apprenticeship showed the same allocation pattern as the four high-stakes documentary sites, the mechanism would not be doing the explanatory work. The paper does not test them empirically and identifies them as the comparison cases a future study should examine. 

Methodological caution: physical frameworks and social analysis. The source corpus contains an inclination to explain institutional phenomena through physical or neurological frameworks. The caution is not a dismissal. Physical findings are relevant where the causal pathway is specified: the finding that prefrontal development continues into the mid-twenties bears on arguments about the timing of educational sorting, provided the argument states how the developmental finding bears on the institutional 

question. What the paper resists is the category error of using a physical framework to do work that requires an institutional explanation. A neurological model does not explain why a dean reads a doctor's note rather than a narrative of coercive control; an audit-pressure account does. The biological-mismatch argument (§7.5) is reframed accordingly: it is an aperture-timing claim, not a neuroscience claim. The institutional point is about the timing of sorting relative to the development of capacity, not about the neural mechanism of capacity itself. Where the framework is offered as a general theory of institutional failure, the paper declines it and relies on the audit, burden, credentialism, and moral-injury literatures. A specific engagement with the reductionism critique in the neuropsychological literature is required before submission and is flagged as outstanding. 

Why widening does not come from within. The counter-cases teach a fifth lesson that belongs here rather than in §10. Correction is local, adversarial, and external. Brown's revision was compelled. Finland's quota was a policy imposed on a selection process. Disability accommodation was built under statutory enforcement. Skills-first hiring emerged from market pressure on the credentialist threshold. In none of these cases did the institution widen its own aperture through internal review. The reason is Step 6 of the mechanism. Widening redistributes interpretive authority, and those who hold authority have an interest in the aperture remaining narrow. Senior researchers' authority is bound to the frameworks the field recognizes; administrators' discretion is bound to the documents their procedures can process. A proposal to widen is therefore not merely a proposal to be more accurate; it is a proposal to redistribute authority, and it is resisted for that reason rather than for the reasons stated. This is why reform arrives as litigation, enforcement, or new infrastructure — and why internal proposals, however well-evidenced, are absorbed or neutralized. The two grade books are the local case of this rule. The teacher who kept them did not persuade the administration to record the composition. She recorded it herself, privately, and the institution's response was to alter its own book. Widening the aperture would have required someone with authority over the record to change what the record contained. No such person was available from inside.

### **§10 — Objections and Responses** 

Objection 1: This is just Scott, Power, and Collins recombined. Response. The paper does not claim to have discovered legibility; it claims to have specified a mechanism of allocation the existing literatures describe within their own domains but do not unify. Scott writes about the state's rendering of populations; Power about rituals of verification; Collins about credential 

stratification. None specifies the three conditions under which documentary verification produces the five-channel reallocation, and none carries the analysis across four sites with a single aperture construct. The finding that detection failure, authentication failure, sequencing failure, and conditioning failure are variants of one pattern is not derivable from any single literature. The separation of prediction from evidential confidence, the specification of counter-configurations as domain boundaries, and the measurement corollary in §3.6 are contributions the cited literatures do not make. The school site (§7) is a contribution the cited literatures do not make at all: it identifies the developmental precondition for the other three sites, which changes the theory from a multi-site analysis to a cycle that begins with the conditioning of the population. 

Objection 2: This is grievance dressed as theory. Response. The loadbearing evidence is documentary and independent of the author: five verified cases with public settlements and a DOJ finding; three ghost-job surveys; a False Claims Act matter; a documented layoff-and-replace episode; for-profit college records; administrative-bloat and aid-absorption data. The witness is quarantined in Appendix C and marked as nonprevalence. And the moral-injury argument, which is the paper's most personal-seeming claim, is not the author's invention: it is made in practitioner-facing venues that warn against recoding occupational betrayal as individual pathology. A reader who rejects every sentence of the witness loses reach but nothing from the argument. The four-stage blackmail architecture (§4.6) is the analytic content of the witness, separable from the witness itself: the four stages and their aperture dependencies are structural predictions, not testimonials, and they would hold if the witness were entirely fictional. 

Objection 3: The evidence is anecdotal. Response. Conceded and reframed. The paper does not establish prevalence, and §4.8, §5.9, and §6.6 say so. But the claim is about mechanism, and mechanism claims are established by demonstrating that the mechanism operates, not by counting instances. A single documented case in which the mechanism is visible and no alternative explanation accounts for the outcome is stronger evidence for a mechanism claim than a thousand cases in which the mechanism is asserted. The paper has five verified such cases at the university site and a comparable documentary base at the corporate site; it does not have them at the publishing site or at the school site, and says so. The Greater Male Variability Hypothesis (§3.3) sharpens the response: the theory predicts that the strongest evidence (the tail cases) will be the rarest in the aggregate record, because the aperture that produces the aggregate also filters the tail. The absence of prevalence data is therefore not evidence against the theory; it is what the theory predicts. 

Objection 4: The alternative is untested. Response. The paper offers no tested alternative in three of four registers (research, pedagogy, policy), and does not claim to. The raw-findings protocol is in Appendix E, marked untested. The engagement-performance alternative in §11.4 is a design principle, not a program. The design principles in §11 are principles, not a tested program. But the labor-market register has a tested alternative: skills-first hiring. The Indeed data — 81 percent of matches via skills algorithms, 5x predictive power, 7 percent higher retention — establishes that aperture widening is feasible at scale and does not produce the collapse in match quality that credentialist screening is meant to prevent. Skills-first is the existence proof. The positive claim is modest but real: the aperture is a design choice (established by the Finnish and Brown countercases); widening is an engineering and political problem rather than a matter of intentions (§9.5); and the conditions under which widening occurs are external pressure and alternative verification (§2.10, §9.2). 

Objection 5: This is a conspiracy theory. Response. The mechanism requires no coordination (§3.5, §8.5). Each administrator makes a locally rational decision under audit pressure; no administrator needs to know what any other is doing. Convergence is the ordinary output of institutional isomorphism. Where the paper describes coordinated behavior — the commercial reference market, the for-profit falsification schemes, the fourstage plagiarism architecture — the coordination is documented at the level of specific firms and specific acts, not inferred as a general condition. The paper nowhere claims a single actor coordinates university, corporation, journal, and school. The kinship reference market (§5.2) requires no coordination beyond reciprocity, which is the structural reason it cannot be prosecuted as conspiracy. The distinction between documented coordination and structural convergence is maintained throughout and is the principal defense. 

Objection 6: Legibility is necessary for coordination. Response. The paper recommends widening, not abandoning. §3.6 and §9.5 both state that legibility is a functional necessity; the claim is that the choice of what counts as legible is contingent and consequential. The disabilityaccommodation literature shows alternative regimes function: documentation from non-standard sources, structured self-report, individualized assessment. The OECD's recognition-of-prior-learning work is a functioning labor-market instance. The Finnish quota shows that adding a criterion can improve allocation. Brown shows a specific aperture can be revised under enforcement, and the courage literature specifies the form the revision must take: a changed record, not a changed sentiment. Skillsfirst shows the labor-market register can be widened at scale. The position is not abolish documentation but specify what the documentation must capture, and build procedures that can capture it. 

Objection 7: You have included cases you have not verified. Response. Correct, and deliberate. The paper uses three registers and marks them at first use. Two reported cases appear in the body: the Hong Kong case, which carries the lawful-aperture argument, and the Louisiana case, which supplies the two-grade-book image. Neither is presented as an established finding of wrongdoing, and neither carries evidentiary weight. Their function is analytical: the verified record consists of institutions that were found to have behaved badly, and it therefore cannot, by itself, distinguish the paper's claim from the claim that some institutions break rules. The Hong Kong case can make that distinction, because the institution was vindicated. The Louisiana case can make the record visible as an object, which the other cases describe but do not show. The paper is explicit about what follows. If both reported cases were withdrawn, the five verified cases, the three verified policy samples, and the corporate record would still establish the asymmetry; what would be lost is the argument that the asymmetry can be lawful and the illustration of the record as an artifact. Both are marked in Appendix D as reported and pending verification. The standard the paper holds others to — do not let the document substitute for the condition — requires it to be equally explicit about its own evidentiary registers, and this objection is the test of whether it has been. The paper's response is to make its own aperture visible (§11.6), which is the strongest possible form of the response: the theory applies to itself, and the paper models the discipline it demands of institutions. 

Objection 8: This is just cynicism. Response. The paper reads as complaint; offers no affirmative pole; the analysis is uniformly negative. The response is that the paper's positive claim is modest but real, and the affirmative pole is the cumulative weight of the alternatives across four registers. Skills-first hiring (labor market): tested at scale, with quantified outcomes. Finnish teacher quota (selection): a documented aperture widening that improved allocation. Brown's post-DOJ revision (mercy): a documented aperture widening compelled by enforcement. Recognition of prior learning (skills validation): a functioning labor-market mechanism. Raw-findings-first protocol (research): untested but specified, with a pilot design available. Engagement-performance alternative (pedagogy): untested but specified, structurally homologous to skills-first and to craft apprenticeship. These are not isolated exceptions; they are instances of the same widening principle across four registers, and the wager of the paper is constructive: understanding the mechanism is the precondition for designing around it. The paper's apparent negativity is the negativity of diagnosis, not of despair. A diagnosis that names the disease is the precondition for any cure; a diagnosis that insists the disease is incurable would be a different paper, and the paper does not make that claim. The wager is that the same widening achieved by litigation at Hunter can be achieved by design everywhere, and the affirmative case is the cumulative 

weight of the alternatives across the four registers in which they have been shown to function.

### **§11 — Implications** 

Stated as design principles, not a program. The paper does not claim any has been tested in three of the four registers (policy, institutional design, research); the labor-market register is the exception, where skills-first hiring has been tested at scale and provides the existence proof. The engagement-performance alternative in §11.4 is included because the theory implies that changing what the aperture reads should change the allocation, and the implication is testable; the paper marks it as untested and proposes a pilot design. 

For policy. Legibility audits. An institution can audit its own aperture by asking, for each allocation rule, what the rule requires a claimant to produce and whether the required document can be produced by a person in the condition the rule is meant to accommodate. The audit is procedural and requires no new data. It would have identified in advance that a sevenday window cannot be met by a person in coercive control and that a fit-tosit doctrine converts submission into a waiver. Trauma-informed timelines. Late notification can be accepted where the delay is itself consistent with the condition, with the burden of demonstrating consistency borne by an assessor rather than the claimant. Brown's revision is a precedent, and the courage literature specifies what the change must be: not a statement of sympathy, but a revised rule governing what the institution will record and how it will show that it did. Proportionate alternative verification. Structured self-report, non-standard documentation, individualized assessment, and recognition of prior learning are administratively feasible and already in use. The principle is that verification should be proportionate to the condition, not that verification should be abandoned. Aid and cost transparency. If only 40 cents of each aid dollar reduces student price, the remaining 60 cents is a documented transfer that the current reporting framework does not make visible. A legibility audit applied to institutional budgets would register the transfer as a channel rather than as an administrative cost. Record integrity. The two grade books and the forprofit falsification schemes point to a principle that is not currently part of standard audit practice: audit the composition, not only the aggregate, and audit the process by which the record was produced. An institution that can show only its official figures has not shown that its figures are accurate; it has shown that it has figures. 

For institutional design. Two-register verification. A standardized register for routine allocation and a second, narrative or interview-based 

register for cases the standardized register cannot capture. The second is more expensive per case; the design question is whether the institution will pay for accuracy where accuracy matters most. Skills-first hiring and recognition of prior learning. Skills-based screening has greater predictive power than credential screening and credential requirements exclude selftaught talent; recognition of prior learning supplies a functioning mechanism. Neither solves the aperture problem — each creates its own verification problems — but both change what must be manufactured and therefore change the incentive structure of §5.2. The direction is already established in practice: where skills-based matching has been adopted at scale, it has not produced the collapse in match quality that credentialist screening is meant to prevent. Skills-first is the master precedent for aperture widening across registers; it is the labor-market analogue of the raw-findings-first protocol (Appendix E) and the engagement-performance alternative (§11.4). Raw-first publication. The protocol proposed in the source corpus — engage with raw findings before reading interpretations; compare interpretations only after direct engagement; propose frameworks only then — is untested and appears in Appendix E. It is included because the theory implies that changing what the aperture reads should change the allocation, and because the implication is testable. The craft-apprenticeship model (§9.3) is its structural analogue: the apprentice learns the material before the field's packaged interpretation is supplied. 

For research. A prevalence study. Comparing extenuating-circumstances outcomes across institutions with different documentary requirements would test whether the aperture, not condition severity, drives the outcome. The study must measure composition, not aggregate rates (§3.6). The two grade books specify the design: wherever possible, obtain both the official record and an independent measure of the underlying distribution, and compare them. A comparison study. The counter-configurations are the comparison cases. If seminar grading and apprenticeship show the same pattern as the high-stakes documentary sites, the mechanism is not doing the explanatory work. An intervention study. A trial in which one institution adopts a second register and a matched institution does not would test whether widening changes allocation. The courage literature supplies the outcome measure: whether the record changed, not whether satisfaction changed. An interest study. Step 6 predicts that internal reform proposals fail at a higher rate than externally imposed ones. The prediction is testable against a sample of institutional reform episodes. An anticipation study. §4.4 predicts that awareness of a documentary requirement suppresses claims before they are filed. The prediction is testable by comparing claim rates across institutions with differing publicity about their documentation rules. A record-integrity study. The two-book structure predicts that where records can be altered, the official aggregate will converge on whatever the 

audit rewards while the underlying composition diverges. The prediction is testable against institutions with varying degrees of record independence. 

The engagement-performance alternative. The source corpus proposed an alternative vision of education that this paper reframes as an aperturewidening proposal in pedagogical register. The principle is the same as in skills-first hiring and raw-findings-first: verify by direct engagement with the material rather than by performance of engagement. The alternative is not romantic. It contains real organizational insights. Planning and strategy roles would teach systems thinking and long-term consequence mapping before abstract theory. Cartography and scouting groups would develop spatial reasoning that textbooks can never teach. Street traffic handling would function as a living physics and mathematics problem, with flow dynamics and congestion patterns experienced directly rather than absorbed passively. Biology and ecology would be learned through direct engagement with living systems rather than through the passive absorption of textbook descriptions. Garbage-cleaning and sustainability crews would develop environmental literacy through the doing of the work rather than through the performance of concern about the work. Planting selfpollinating fruit and nut trees, with berries growing everywhere, would create food abundance from actual ecological understanding. The soil would improve from the cleanup crews, the food would grow in abundance, and the virtuous cycle — better soil producing more abundance, which rewards those who invested in it — would replace the vicious cycle of scarcity and hoarding that the school produces. 

The deeper insight is that modern education treats humans as empty vessels to be filled, while the engagement-performance alternative describes a system of apprenticeship in which students earn their expertise through direct engagement with real problems. The hoarding behavior the school produces emerges because scarcity is built into the economic system the school feeds into. The alternative creates abundance — food, soil quality, ecological understanding, real skill — which then rewards those who invested in it. This is exactly the opposite dynamic from competitive education, which produces scarcity mindset and hoarding. The skills-first movement in the labor market, the raw-findings-first protocol in science, and the engagement-performance alternative in pedagogy are all expressions of the same corrective: bypass the inherited signal, engage directly with the underlying reality, form judgment from the engagement, and reward the engagement rather than the performance of engagement. The same wound runs through every tissue. The same medicine would heal them all. The medicine is not proposed here as a tested program. The diagnosis is. 

Conclusion. This paper began with a student who was hospitalized for a suicide attempt and returned to find her locks changed. It ends with a 

general claim about how formal institutions allocate. The claim is that institutions allocate by what they can see, and that what they can see is determined by what their instruments, procedures, and principals can register. The construct is the aperture. The pattern that emerges when the aperture is narrower than the purpose is legibility bias. The pattern produces five channels of reallocation — risk, credential value, labor, moral liability, economic surplus — and the paper has traced them across a university site and a corporate site, illustratively across a publishing site, and across a school site that is the developmental precondition for the other three. 

Two images carry the argument. The first is the aperture diagram: purpose above, aperture below, populations sorting in the gap. The second is a pair of grade books, one private and accurate, one official and altered. The first is the theory; the second is the theory in a drawer. The paper's claim is that the second image is not an anomaly but the ordinary form of the first — and that the same structure recurs whenever an institution's obligation runs to a record rather than to the thing the record describes. The arrangement does not merely fail to measure value. It expels real value — honest talent into unmeasured informal work, at 58 percent of global employment — and retains fake value — manufactured references, ghost demand, inflated placement statistics, absorbed aid — and it cannot detect either movement because both are invisible to an aperture calibrated to documents. 

The mechanism is structural in its first five steps and interest-based in its sixth. It is the aggregate output of locally rational decisions under audit pressure, reinforced by the interest of those who control the interpretive categories in keeping them narrow. This is why the pattern is stable, why it survives the good intentions of individual administrators, why it does not require conspirators, and why reform arrives from outside — as litigation, enforcement, or new infrastructure — rather than from internal review. It is also why the pattern reproduces: an aperture that rewards manufacture and filters honesty will, over iterations, select for manufacturers, because the honest have been removed from the pool it can see. And it is why the pattern begins with the school: the school conditions the population to accept the aperture as natural, and the conditioned population is the population the university, the corporation, and the journal then sort. 

It is also why the pattern is contingent. Brown revised its aperture under DOJ enforcement, and the courage literature explains why the revision counts: it changed the record. Finland corrected a defective selection process. Disability accommodation regimes accept alternative verification. Recognition of prior learning functions in labor markets. Skills-first hiring has been adopted at scale. The aperture is a design choice, and design choices can be changed — but the change must be to what the institution 

records, not to how it feels about what it records. The limits of the argument are the limits of its evidence. The university and corporate sites are documented; publishing is illustrative; the school site is supported by architectural observation and the shadow-education literature. Two cases are reported rather than verified, and they are marked. The cases establish that the mechanism operates; they do not establish how often. The witness is quarantined because it illustrates and does not prove. The alternatives are untested in three of four registers, and the paper says so wherever it mentions them. The measurement corollary is the paper's own most stringent demand on itself: the pattern it describes is invisible in aggregate rates, and the paper offers no aggregate evidence for it — only the composition of its case record. 

The self-application coda. The paper has its own aperture: the verified / reported / witnessed evidentiary registers. Its own evidence is filtered through what its instruments can verify. This is stated explicitly as the strongest answer to the seventh objection. The paper models the discipline it demands of institutions: making one's own aperture visible is the precondition for changing it. The paper's own aggregate — eleven cases, seventeen angles, five channels, four sites — conceals its own composition: which cases are verified (eight of eleven), which angles are diagnostic (seventeen of seventeen, but each is a lens rather than a claim), which channels are site-derived (the first three are within-site; the last two are arrangement-level). The paper does not claim to have escaped its own aperture. It claims to have made it visible. The task is not to abandon legibility. Institutions cannot function without it. The task is to widen the aperture — to build verification regimes that can register conditions the current instruments cannot detect, and that do not reward the manufacture of evidence while punishing the inability to produce it. The Hunter student's case ended in a settlement and a policy revision. That is one aperture, widened by litigation. The wager of this paper is that the same widening can be achieved by design across four registers, and that understanding the mechanism is the precondition for designing it. The wager includes the wager that making one's own aperture visible is the precondition for changing it. The paper holds itself to that standard.

### **Appendix C — Witness Method Note** 

The witness material was collected during the author's tenure as a tutor and university student. It is presented as autoethnographic evidence, not a 

systematic study; it illustrates mechanisms and does not establish prevalence. It includes: the reassignment to a role with no duties while salary continued; the refusal of a learning-deficit mapping proposal; the demand for staged photographic materials; the intimidation that followed refusal; the coordinator whose posted role description exceeded the role performed; the deliberate introduction of plagiarized material into group submissions to create shared liability; the library and social-setting cohorts mapping high-demand niches and arranging references through family and peer businesses; and the observation of research participation as display rather than engagement. 

Three details deserve separate statement because they are the appendix's evidentiary content rather than its atmosphere. First, the staged employment was staged before an audience: staff, cleaners, and recruiters were positioned so that they could attest that the person had been seen in the building. This is the fissure entity's substitute for an employment record — where a real employer produces a payroll entry, the fissure entity produces witnesses, and witnesses are cheaper. The fabricated role was consistently a recruiting consultancy position, chosen because it is plausible, high-status, and structurally unverifiable: the work product is conversations, and conversations leave no artifact. Second, the absorbed labor was performed in the expectation of recognition. Duties belonging to a designated coordinator — matching students to tutors, checking tutors' skills, proposing operational changes, closing windows — were taken up in the expectation of eventual promotion. The expectation was not met; the performance was read as exceeding the role, and the ground of the objection was not that the duties were neglected but that they were visibly performed by someone else. Competence became a threat in the precise measure that it made incompetence legible. Third, the observation of research participation was made over an extended period across repeated program entries, and it is the basis for the display-signature claim in §3.3; it is not offered as a prevalence estimate. 

Three further specifics are added here because they are the analytic content of the witness, separable from the witness itself, and they map onto specific sections of the paper. The first is the gap-mapping proposal: fifthgrade students whose mathematics skills were lacking from third or fourth grade. The witness proposed to map the deficits and coordinate with the director to fill the gaps before attempting to teach the grade-level material. The proposal was refused for reputation-loss reasons; the witness was reassigned to an empty room. This is the micro-form of the shadoweducation feedback loop (§4.6): honest gap-mapping would have reduced the demand for the remediation market, and the institution's revenue depended on the gap remaining unmapped. The second is the stagedpromotional-photograph demand: the witness was asked to grab a student from the hallways and pose with open books, to fabricate the illusion of 

active high-quality learning. The witness refused on the grounds of integrity, stating that he was a person of integrity and would not do it. The company's response was intimidation and reverse accusation: the witness was accused of attacking the company's integrity. This is the four-stage blackmail architecture (§4.6) in its preliminary form: the institution attempts to manufacture the appearance, the witness refuses, the institution neutralizes the witness through reframing. The third is the LinkedIn fiction quote: the coordinator whose role was not even closing windows posted on LinkedIn a role description involving continuous agilelike reevaluation environments and continuous contact with all the stakeholders involved. This is the display signature (§3.3) in its corporatephase form: the energy invested in the LinkedIn description is energy not invested in the underlying role, and the description is what the corporate aperture reads.

### **Appendix E — Raw-Findings Protocol (Untested)** 

Three stages, from the source corpus, presented as proposal. Stage 1 (months 0–6): consume only raw data, code repositories, methodological notes, and quality-control procedures; avoid abstracts, conclusions, and review articles. Stage 2 (months 6–18): read existing interpretations and compare them against direct engagement with the data. Stage 3 (month 18+): propose frameworks. Three obstacles make adoption difficult, and a pilot must confront them. First, time: immersion requires months before any publishable output, which is incompatible with the publication timelines that govern doctoral funding and hiring. Second, materials: training is built around packaged knowledge because packaged knowledge is efficient; a raw-first curriculum would require rebuilding reading lists, qualifying examinations, and seminar structure. Third, interest: those who control the field's interpretive categories have an interest in those categories remaining authoritative, and a protocol that requires newcomers to form judgments before receiving the categories is a protocol that redistributes interpretive authority. The third obstacle is Step 6 (§3.5) in its most local form, and it is the reason a pilot will be easier to run outside a department whose senior faculty are invested in the current frame than inside one. 

A pilot would randomize entering doctoral cohorts to protocol versus standard socialization and measure the divergence of their first independent research questions from the field's dominant frame. The paper does not claim the protocol works. The protocol is structurally homologous to skills-first hiring (§5.1, §11.2) and to the engagement-performance alternative in pedagogy (§11.4): all three are instances of the same widening principle, applied in different registers. The craft-apprenticeship model (§9.3) is the structural basis for all three: the apprentice learns the material before the field's packaged interpretation is supplied. 

### **Appendix F — The Seventeen Angles as Diagnostic Taxonomy** 

The seventeen angles recovered from the source critique function as a diagnostic taxonomy complementary to the five channels. The channels describe what moves; the angles describe where to look for the movement. Each angle maps to one or more channels, and several specify behavioral signatures the channels abstract away. The angles are presented as a reference for researchers encountering the failure mode in domains this paper does not analyze in depth. 

Angle 1: Streetlight Effect. Researchers look for what is missing only where it is easiest to measure. Maps to the aperture construct (§3.4) 

directly. Behavioral signature: outcome studies understate the aperture's reach because they measure the claims that were made, not the claims that were abandoned. Angle 2: Credentialist Bias. Self-taught talent is invisible to credentialist screening. Maps to the corporate channel (§5.1). Behavioral signature: the credential becomes the entry threshold rather than the signal of competence. Angle 3: Alternative Credentialing. GitHub portfolios and open-source contributions are productive but unmeasured. Maps to the labor channel (§3.3, §5.7). Behavioral signature: the productive actor is invisible to the credentialist screening process. Angle 4: Informal Leadership. Community organizers are invisible. Maps to the moral-liability channel (§3.3). Behavioral signature: leadership demonstrated in informal settings has no economic metric. Angle 5: Narrow Productivity. Wages as flawed proxy. Maps to the measurement corollary (§3.6). Behavioral signature: market value conflated with productive output. 

Angle 6: Greater Male Variability Hypothesis. Average effects mask distributional losses at the extreme tail. Supplies the statistical mechanism for the tail-concealment claim (§3.3, §3.6). Behavioral signature: the most skilled exiters are the least visible, because their exits are read as outliers rather than as the predicted population. Angle 7: Paradox of Gender Quotas. Quotas can create paradoxical effects not captured by engagement metrics. Resolved by the Finnish case (§9.1). Behavioral signature: postquota performance may decline relative to pre-quota, but the decline is in the population the aperture cannot register. Angle 8: Self-Stigmatization. The apertured population internalizes the aperture's verdict. Behavioral signature: the apertured reduce their willingness to compete. Angle 9: The Quota Ghost. The threat of an aperture change alters behavior before any change occurs. Behavioral signature: managers perceive quotas as a threat to meritocracy and reduce engagement in anticipation. 

Angle 10: Spillover Effects. The aperture degrades signals for the entire category, not only for those filtered. Maps to §5.2 spillover cost. Behavioral signature: honest candidates bear a verification cost that the manufacturing candidate imposed. Angle 11: Network Displacement. The apertured lose access to networks controlled by the unapertured. Maps to §5.7 informal economy. Behavioral signature: male networks have historically provided more benefits than female networks; the aperture does not measure social capital. Angle 12: Efficiency Loss. Effort declines under apertured conditions. Behavioral signature: effort levels are 12 percentage points lower under a quota in laboratory experiments. Angle 13: Backlash. Hostility toward those who expose the aperture's operation. Maps to the neutralization phase (§4.6, §8.3). Behavioral signature: women in competitive environments with quotas and peer sabotage are the prime victims of sabotage. 

Angle 14: The Meritocracy Assumption. The aperture's defenders assume the pre-aperture measure was accurate. Resolved by the Finnish case (§9.1). Behavioral signature: the no-loss claim assumes the pre-quota measure of merit is accurate. Angle 15: Unpaid Labor. Caregiving invisible. Maps to the labor channel (§3.3). Behavioral signature: a man who becomes a primary caregiver is performing a vital productive role, but is registered as disengaged. Angle 16: Self-Selection. The apertured and the unapertured select different strategies. Behavioral signature: women more likely to undertake data entry; men predominantly engage in driving, delivery, and trading gigs. Angle 17: Future Uncertainty. Engagement is dynamic. Behavioral signature: a man who is engaged today may become disengaged tomorrow in response to a quota policy. 

The seventeen angles do not falsify the five channels; they diagnose the same failure mode from different observation points. A researcher studying the spillover effect of fake references on honest candidates (Angle 10) is studying the credential-value channel; the angle specifies where to look, the channel specifies what moves. The angles also preserve the original critique's specificity against the gender-quota research, which the fivechannel abstraction compresses but does not eliminate. The metaapplication in §3.9 and §8.4 — that the no-loss claim is itself legibility bias operating inside the research — is the angle diagnostic applied to the research that produced the angles.
